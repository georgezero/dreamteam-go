## 1. Owner decisions and goals

### 1.1 Binding owner decisions (quoted)

> 1. Contracted hours: YES, mandatory for hourly employees. Each hourly employee
> has a contractual weekly-hours commitment alongside the actual roster.
> - Contracted hours determine minimum compensation and notice/severance
>   obligations.
> - Rostered hours determine planned coverage.
> - Worked hours determine actual utilization and overtime calculations.
> A player cannot reduce a 40-hour employee to zero scheduled hours and
> eliminate payroll costs without formally terminating. Use guaranteed weekly
> base pay, with actual worked hours and applicable overtime accounted for
> without double-paying the same hours. Notice pay and severance use the
> contracted weekly baseline. Contract changes are NOT a separate V0.2 player
> action: hire establishes the contract, terminate ends it, set_roster cannot
> silently change it.

> 2. Evaluation: T90 is the primary management evaluation. T30 is early
> operational and financial feedback; T180 is optional longer-term validation.
> Use a transparent metric vector, not a single weighted score: operating
> margin, cash/financing position, completed exams, lost demand, access
> delays, urgent TAT breaches, routine TAT, workforce obligations. Set minimum
> service gates so a player cannot look successful by eliminating staff,
> refusing patients or postponing reports. Gates are grounded in the
> scenario's measured achievable baseline, not arbitrary perfection.

> 3. Radiologist roster edits: EXCLUDED from V0.2. set_roster applies only to
> hourly technologists and front-desk employees. Radiologist coverage is
> managed by set_reading_allocation and contract_teleradiology, with clearly
> defined costs (do not mix salaried allocation with hourly shift coverage).

> 4. No-action neutrality: for existing V0.1 scenarios with no V0.2 actions,
> patient flow, reporting, accounting, logs and observable outcomes are
> unchanged. New staff-lifecycle fields may legitimately change the state
> digest and therefore the run digest; record and regenerate those
> differences explicitly (the step 18.1 precedent). Preserve the original
> golden references as historical compatibility fixtures rather than
> silently overwriting them.

> 5. Session schema: new sessions use v2 (new action types and lifecycle
> events). Keep a v1 reader that continues to support authoritative replay of
> existing records under documented compatibility rules. Do not promise that
> old state digests match a new engine's state schema; report outcome
> compatibility separately.

> 6. Feasibility sweep: mandatory Step 0 and a HARD GATE before any action code.
> Static scenario overlays representing the eventual steady-state effects of
> the actions. If a profitable practice is not possible with only the
> documented staffing, demand, template and reading-allocation levers, stop
> and revisit the design.

### 1.2 Goals and preserved foundations

V0.2 supplies five management actions: `hire`, `terminate`, `set_roster`,
`set_reading_allocation`, `contract_teleradiology`. It adds an employee lifecycle,
a separate candidate `sustainable` scenario, and a T90 evaluation vector with
service eligibility gates. Profitability and marginal hiring opportunities are
hypotheses until measured; neither +6–12% margin nor a particular backlog is a
release requirement.

Preserve integer time/money, practice-local time without DST, origin-keyed
randomness, timestamp transactions, authoritative replay, immutable scenario
inputs, and the observation boundary. Preserve existing V0.1 action semantics
in the legacy path, including action messages and serialization. Keep
`established` the struggling default and `mini` a compatibility fixture.
Revenue stays global billing at report final; service quality does not directly
change reimbursement or referrals in V0.2. Preserve existing worklist ordering,
including urgent protection and the default policy's promotion of overdue work
(`internal/sim/reading.go:108`); do not silently redefine that policy.

Radiologist hiring, salary negotiation, employee contract amendments, absences,
fatigue, equipment expansion, new opening hours, virtual supervision, template
editing by players, and discretionary patient rescheduling are outside V0.2.
Practice-caused displacement is required now because staffing actions need it.
Static template changes in Step 0 are scenario authoring, not a sixth action.

## 3. Measured problem and corrected unit economics

Nine-seed V0.1 measurements: 30-day margin −12.5%, 90-day margin −14.0%; about
1,314–1,381 exams per 30.4-day month from about 1,650 monthly orders and
walk-ins. Adding three full-time technologists reduces booking losses but
produces a 90-day margin of −18.6%. The three-tech T30 result is $223.2k revenue,
$260.4k expense and −$37.2k profit. Source: `docs/calibration/step-18-report.md`
and `docs/calibration/results/summary.md`. The latter reports exams per **30**
days; do not mix that normalization with the report's 30.4-day month.

The payer outcomes are mutually exclusive, with expected collection fraction
`(1 − denied − short) + short × meanShort + denied × recovered`
(`internal/sim/finance.go:60`). The input mix gives net/base = **1.13305**.

of the completed case mix:

At 173 paid hours/month and 25% benefits: XR $36/h costs $7,785 (about 184 extra
completed-and-reported XR exams); US $44/h costs $9,515 (61 US); MR $45/h costs
$9,731 (36 MR). Recruitment, training, overtime and reading can raise these
break-even quantities. Three additional $38/$44/$45 technicians cost about
$27,464/month. Fixed monthly expenses are $88,500. Cutting the combined
radiologist allocation by ten percentage points saves about $5,000/month:
`$480,000 × 0.10 / 12 × 1.25`.

### 3.1 Employed versus outsourced reading

At 60%/50%, employed radiologists cost approximately $55,000/month including
benefits, or $40 per report at 1,379 monthly reports. Draft 1's illustrative
outsourced mix averages approximately $29.31/read **before retainers and night
fees**. Thus the claimed outsourcing price guard was false. Salary is a fixed
site obligation while its allocation is in force; the immediate marginal wage
cost of another in-house read is zero. Allocation changes make a portion of
salary avoidable only after notice. Compare:

`avoidable allocation salary + benefits` versus
`incremental retainer + fees for actual outsourced mix + night fees`.

Hold workload and service eligibility constant. Include retained salary,
capacity limits, handoff delay, and unfinished studies. Cheap outsourcing can
be a valid choice. There is no artificial price floor, revenue bonus, or
unmeasured clinical-quality penalty. §8 specifies operational quality limits;
a post-implementation allocation × contract experiment must test substitution.

**Part-time cells were blocked (resolved in Step 0C, §4.7).**
`internal/scenario/validate.go:317` required
`shift.start + maxShiftHours < 24:00`; with the 12-hour cap no shift could start
after 12:00, so 16:00–20:00 was rejected. Changing the global cap would
invalidate existing 8.5-hour shifts. Resolving this needs a narrow per-shift or
per-employee cap (e.g. `maxWorkUntil` 23:00) used by validation and runtime
hold-over, with unchanged defaults and neutrality evidence; that is non-action

a 4-hour instead of an 8-hour paid evening shift saves ≈$4.9k per 30 days per
technologist, so the best measured configuration could at most reach about

### 4.4 Gate decision rule and candidate handoff

A candidate is feasible only if, at twenty seeds:

1. Mean **and median** T90 operating profit are positive after any contractual
   guarantee adjustment; mean days-31–90 profit is also positive. Publish the
   fraction of individually profitable seeds and the lower confidence bound;
   do not translate a positive mean into a guarantee for each seed.
2. Its service vector passes the baseline-relative limits in §13 using a
   predeclared matched no-action reference (same demand and inputs, baseline
   roster/templates/allocation). Freeze the tolerances from baseline variability
   before selecting the winner. Reading backlog/overdue growth must pass too.
3. Cash/obligations reconcile; any financing requirement is explicit. A result
   requiring unmodelled free credit is not a sustainable release candidate.
4. A neighboring configuration leaves a measured useful management opportunity
   at takeover: improved service at a stated affordable cost, or increased
   profit without failing gates. Publish that comparison. Do not ship every
   profitable marginal hire already installed and advertise it as a decision.

Step 0 passes only with a complete, reproducible report satisfying these rules
within the listed levers. Failure to find profitable service-eligible operation
is **NO GO: stop and revisit the premise**, not permission to change prices,
behavior or durations. Blocked cells or unfilled gates leave Step 0 pending.
The old 6–12% target is not imposed or presumed. Scenario-specific coverage and
service bands replace the old MR-dark-after-16:30 and forced-backlog targets.

After a pass, select a separate `sustainable` overlay with its own fingerprint,
warm-up and no-action baseline. Validate the actual five-action T90 plan later,
including recruitment and termination costs. T180 is optional validation;
T90 is mandatory. Static success alone does not establish interesting gameplay.

### 4.6 Step 0B: CT/XR booking losses and templates

Owner decision (2026-10-15): investigate why CT/XR referrals are lost while
their rooms are ≈40% used, and add static CT/XR templates as a permitted
lever. The fixes for the noon shift-start limit and teleradiology stay separate.
Method, tables and reproduction: `docs/calibration/feasibility/step0b/`.

**Diagnosis.** A passive booking-loss probe classifies every compatible slot
in each lost patient's acceptance window by the first FirstFit check it fails.
It assigns one primary cause by a documented precedence: shared technologist,
not rostered, no supervision, notice, template category, category full, no
template slot. It also records every cause present. Production change: one
function field on the state, excluded from the state digest like the
existing test hooks, and one nil-checked call in `offer`. Neutrality:
`TestLossProbeIsPassive` shows log, ledger, state and action digests
unchanged, and the 20-seed Step 0 candidate reproduces exactly with the probe
on.

At the Step 0 best candidate, 72% of lost CT/XR referrals (CT 135 of 197, XR
90 of 116 per 90 days) had a free, staffed, compatible slot in their window,
blocked because the **one technologist covering both rooms** was committed:
Riley all Monday and Tuesday–Friday mornings, Sam from 16:15. 92% of them
were urgent or soon patients (tolerance 0–10 days). The template category mix
explains ≈7% of CT losses. US losses are unstaffed US slots; MR losses (with
the evening technologist) are template mix and full templates.

**Template and coverage variants** (9 paired seeds, on the Step 0 best
candidate; 20 seeds for the best):

|---|---:|---:|---|
| 12-minute XR slots (`XR12`) | +0.5% (20 seeds; prior +0.4%) | +0.4; paired +$1.16k per 90 d, CI +0.36 to +1.96 | −45 referral losses, +39 walk-ins leaving; exams unchanged |

**Verdict: the gate still fails.** Templates are not the binding constraint

shared-technologist staffing; low XR contribution (≈$42 per exam against

(more evening imaging means next-morning reports); and fixed costs with
salaried reading. The best permitted static configuration is now the Step 0
candidate with 12-minute XR slots: +0.5% mean T90 margin (−0.6 to +2.9), T90
profit +$3.9k (95% CI +$0.8k to +$7.0k), 14 of 20 seeds profitable. That is
consistently near zero, not materially profitable.

### 4.7 Step 0C: staffing architectures

Owner decision (option G, 2026-10-15): stop adjusting the struggling
practice's staffing and test explicitly different, fully costed coverage
architectures for a larger practice. Method, tables and reproduction:
`docs/calibration/feasibility/step0c/`.

**Prerequisite: late shifts.** A shift's cap (holdover included) is now the
earlier of start + `maxShiftHours` and accounting close, 23:59:59
(`sim.Policies.ShiftCap`); validation rejects a shift longer than the cap or
ending after accounting close, not one starting after noon. This replaces
the proposed `maxWorkUntil` field: no new scenario field, and it only
differs from the old rule for shifts that were invalid before. Regression
tests (written failing first): 16:00–20:00, 17:00–20:00, 11:30–20:00 and
19:00–23:59 valid; over-cap and past-midnight shifts rejected; late shifts
run with their room staffed and every engine invariant holding
(`TestShortLateShifts`, `TestShiftCap`, `TestShortLateShiftsRun`).
`TestRegress05MidnightBoundary` now runs its 12:00–23:45 fixture: the late
patient is rescheduled because the exam cannot finish before accounting
close, and nobody works past midnight. Goldens, replay and determinism tests
are unchanged; the 20-seed Step 0B candidate reproduces exactly.

**Where the conflicts were.** At the Step 0B best configuration, 62% of CT
shared-technologist losses fall on Monday (Riley staffs X-ray and CT alone,
mostly during the 12:00–16:00 contrast block); the rest fall on Tuesday–Friday
evenings (Sam alone) and, less, mornings.

**Architectures** (×1.30 demand, 12-minute XR slots; every new employee at
$38/h with 25% benefits; contracted hours = rostered paid hours): A shared
(control); B dedicated XR and CT day core with a part-time shared evening
(Sam 16:00–20:00, Monday evening newly staffed); C B plus part-time dedicated
evenings; D a part-time float in the measured conflict periods (Monday on CT,
Tue–Fri evenings on X-ray); E full-time pairs of B–D with the same coverage;
each with the evening MR technologist full-time (11:30–20:00) or part-time
(16:00–20:00); and the radiologists' site allocation at 60/50 (base),
62.5/52.5, 65/55 and 70/60.

**Findings** (9 paired seeds, 20 for the leaders):

* A part-time MR evening is worth +$4.9k a month with no volume change.
* Dedicated or float coverage removes 80–95% of the shared-technologist
  losses (+57 to +90 exams a month, −125 to −173 lost referrals in 90 days).
* Part-time beats full-time for the same qualified coverage in every pair,
  by $2.6–7.8k a month.
* The recovered volume overwhelms reading at the base allocation (urgent p90
  188 → 1,046 minutes, urgent breach rate 10% → 29%). Restoring it costs
  ≈$2.5k a month per 2.5 allocation points.

## 5. Staff registry, contracts and lifecycle

### 5.1 Authoritative registry and history

Current employees point into `cfg.Employees` (`internal/sim/world.go:83`);
plans read that array (`internal/sim/config.go:352`); metrics allocate and
subtract fixed employee slices (`internal/sim/metrics.go:75`,
`internal/sim/metrics.go:369`). V0.2 requires a registry **separate from immutable
scenario config**. Copy initial employee terms into registry records; mutable
roster/allocation/lifecycle versions belong to state and are digested. Do not
append to configuration or retain pointers into a reallocating slice.

Registry indices are append-only; departed employees and IDs remain. Initial
order is scenario order. New public IDs are the first unused `H00001`, …,
skipping every ID already present, including arbitrary scenario IDs. Allocate
only on accepted hire commit; preview/rejection allocates nothing. Store the
accepted batch/action identity and hire `requestId` on the employee. Repeating
that request with identical content is `unchanged`, even after departure;
different content is `request_conflict`.

Replace the fixed `uint64` staff mask (`internal/sim/demand.go:123`) with an
ordered growable bit set, retaining ascending registry-index iteration. Do not
reuse terminated bits or impose a lifetime recruitment limit. **[Proposed]**
concurrent caps are eight technologists and four desk employees; recruiting,
onboarding, active and notice all count, terminated do not. Existing scenarios
above a cap can load; additional hires reject until below it. Cap values are
scenario policy, with allocation/overflow bounds checked before append.
Remote contract IDs similarly use the first unused `T00001`, …, never recycled.
Request IDs are namespaced by action type. Per-entity plan revision numbers
increase only on committed changes; each version also records its accepting
batch/action ID. Version removals retain tombstones for audit and stale events.

Snapshots store registry length and cumulative values by stable index. An
employee absent from an older snapshot contributes zero; historical output lists
only employees disclosed by that period's end. Employment/roster metadata is
queried as of that end. Cached and uncached historical summaries must agree
before and after a hire. Keep departed employees in periods where they worked
or incurred obligations; current headcount never determines old array indexing.

### 5.2 Three distinct hours and compatibility migration

| Quantity | Definition | Drives |
|---|---|---|
| Contracted | Immutable weekly paid-hours commitment, `contractedWeeklyMinutes`; excludes overtime | Guaranteed base wages, notice baseline, severance |
| Rostered | Net planned shift time after unpaid breaks, including separately identified approved extensions | Future coverage and expected payroll above guarantee |
| Worked | Actual paid on-duty time, including idle availability, training and hold-over; excludes breaks taken | Utilization denominator, actual base pay, weekly overtime |

Busy task time is a subset of worked time; paid guarantee shortfall is **not**
worked time and creates no coverage or overtime. Training is worked paid time
but supplies no patient-service capacity. A zero-hour roster leaves the contract
and its payroll obligations intact. **[Proposed]** new hires and their later
regular rosters may not exceed contracted net minutes in either rota week;
approved dated extensions and actual hold-over are separately paid excess.
This prevents a nominal one-minute contract from carrying a recurring full-time
roster. Legacy unedited patterns retain their original A/B maxima; edited
patterns may not exceed the greater of the frozen original week total and C.
A part-time contract cannot become a recurring full-time roster without a new
employment contract, which V0.2 can establish only through termination/hire.

New hourly hires require 1–2,400 contracted minutes/week **[proposed]**. Every
initial hourly employee also has a contract. For a legacy scenario without the
field, infer the smaller net roster total of its two rota weeks, frozen at
load; do not assume every employee has 40 hours. In the supplied base, E01/E03/
E05 infer 40 h; E04 and E06 infer **39.5 h** (Saturday is 7.5 paid hours).
A legacy employee with no weekly duty may infer zero; an explicit V0.2 hire
cannot. Publish the inferred terms as metadata, never rewrite the scenario.

For partial weeks of legacy employment at the epoch, distribute that frozen
weekly guarantee across the original contract's rota weekdays in proportion to
net original roster minutes. These weights are immutable **contract accrual
weights**, not later roster edits. New contracts use equal 1/7 calendar-day
weights. Full weeks always guarantee C hours. This migration avoids inventing
Monday wages before a no-warm-up Tuesday starts and avoids extra no-action
postings for legacy patterns. §11 requires actual evidence of neutral postings,
not an exemption from guaranteed compensation after a roster edit.

### 5.3 Lifecycle and onboarding

`recruiting → onboarding → active → notice → terminated` is the display path.
Internally notice is an irrevocable termination overlay with `serviceStage`
(recruiting/onboarding/active) preserved until the end. This allows termination
of a pending hire or trainee without pretending they are productive.

| Stage | Contract/pay | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiting, accepted hire until startDate 00:00 | Recruiting fee recognized; future start/notice/severance commitment disclosed | Future active capacity only after computed training completion |
| Onboarding | Guarantee begins at startDate; actual paid training counts toward weekly worked hours | None: no independent registration, room staffing or contrast supervision |
| Active | Guaranteed wages plus worked excess/premium | Qualified roster coverage |
| Notice | Same terms until exclusive termination boundary | Same eligibility as underlying service stage; scheduled staff work normally |
| Terminated | No new service accrual; all earned pay, severance and unpaid bills survive | None; in-flight on-site work must already have ended by the preceding accounting close |

**[Proposed]** recruiting lead: technician 28 calendar days, desk 14; the premium
pay tier shortens it by seven days. No hidden candidate draws. Training requires
**40 attended paid hours** for a technician, **24** for desk, counted only in
rostered training intervals after breaks. No extension can accelerate training.
A provider-led orientation/preceptorship is included in the recruiting fee;
there is no modeled productive employee mentor or phantom shadow capacity.
Completion at a shift end enables independent work only from the next midnight.
Five tiny shifts cannot substitute for forty hours.

The deterministic training plan must complete within sixty calendar days of
start. Hire and subsequent roster edits validate the complete plan; projected
completion is visible and used in future bookability. A change that delays
completion reconciles every affected booking (§7). Invalid/incomplete training
plans reject; active employees may have an empty roster and still receive pay.
Initial scenario employees are active/trained from the epoch. Static sweep
hires likewise represent trained steady-state employees.

Termination before start is supported through `terminate`: lastDay must be at
least fourteen days after both the request date and contractual startDate.
The recruiting fee is non-refundable, guarantee starts on startDate even if the
roster is removed, and two weeks' severance remains due. There is no free
pending-hire cancellation path. After accepting termination, neither withdrawing
notice nor moving lastDay is supported in V0.2; identical repeats are no-ops.

### 5.4 Payroll arithmetic, notice and severance

Let C be the contract guarantee for a complete week, W actual worked hours,
r the hourly rate, and P = max(0, W − 40). The weekly economic wage obligation is:

`base = r × max(C, W)`

`premium = r × 0.5 × P`

`wages = base + premium`, with benefits `25% × wages` for the supplied scenario.

Use the scenario's weekly threshold/factor, not hardcoded values. The examples
use 40 hours and 1.5×. A premium is an additional 0.5× over base already paid;
guarantee and worked base never both pay the same hour. Partial employment
weeks prorate **only C** by frozen contractual weights on employed dates.
Never multiply actual worked wages by days employed a second time. Notice is
ordinary continued guarantee/worked compensation, not a second wage stream.
Severance = `2 × r × full contractual weekly hours`, plus benefits; no assumed
overtime, and no dependence on the last roster.

Preserve existing per-person/day actual-time postings and `HourlyPay` rounding
(`internal/sim/finance.go:362`). Maintain cumulative base-work cents B and earned
guarantee cents G for the week. At each day close, guarantee provision is
`Q = max(0, G − B)`. Post only `Q_new − Q_old`, signed; a decrease credits wage
expense and debits provisional guaranteed payroll as worked pay replaces the
guarantee. Hold this provision outside ordinary accrued payroll until weekly
finalization (§9); a Friday payday must not settle a still-reversible provision.
Use separate keys for these adjustments, never reverse ordinary worked pay.
G uses cumulative integer rounding; legacy day wages retain existing truncation.
For cumulative worked base B, use the sum of daily base-at-regular-rate cents,
excluding premium; keep premium cents separately under the existing `HourlyPay`
result so the total retains its daily rounding. Benefits use the scenario's
existing nearest-cent rule (`internal/sim/finance.go:355`).
At Sunday close or termination, reconcile the guarantee and benefits to cents,
then reset weekly counters Monday. Zero adjustments produce no ledger or log
entry. Actual wages and the net guarantee provision together implement the
formula, subject to the documented daily cent rounding. Benefits follow actual
postings at the current rule; provision benefits are a cumulative balance
adjustment so reversals cannot leave residual benefits.

| Worked example | Wage arithmetic | Benefits at 25% | Total |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| C=40 h, W=0, r=$40 | $1,600 guarantee, $0 worked/premium | $400 | $2,000/week |
| C=40, W=32 | $1,280 worked base + $320 guarantee shortfall | $400 | $2,000/week |
| C=40, W=45 | $1,800 base + $100 premium on five hours | $475 | $2,375/week |
| C=20, W=28 | $1,120 worked base, no guarantee top-up, no premium | $280 | $1,400/week |
| New C=40, employed Wed–Sun, W=16 | Guarantee 40×5/7 h: $1,142.85 at cumulative cent truncation; worked $640 replaces that amount, top-up $502.85 | $285.71 | $1,428.56 |
| C=40, two full notice weeks at W=0, then termination | $3,200 notice wages + $3,200 severance | $1,600 combined | $8,000; no third payment for the same notice hours |

Payroll liabilities settle on the existing anchored biweekly payday, including
after departure (`internal/sim/finance.go:282`). Severance is recognized when
termination is accepted (unconditional obligation) and paid on the first payday
after lastDay. It need not fall inside T90; §13 counts outstanding commitments.
Worked time this week, accrued payroll, and prior claims survive warm-up and
employee departure. Do not reset them at takeover or on a roster change.

## 6. Five actions: API, validation and previews

### 6.1 Common contract and atomic batches

`api.Action`, `World.Apply`, `Session.Apply` remain the public action path;
`ValidateBatch` is added, with `Validate` a one-element wrapper. The present
implementation validates/applies sequentially (`internal/sim/actions.go:44`);
**atomic V0.2 planning is new behavior**, not an existing capability.

A batch containing any V0.2 action must contain only the five types below.
Mixing legacy actions rejects with `mixed_batch`; execute legacy policy/extension
batches separately at the same timestamp if needed. Legacy-only batches retain
V0.1 sequential rejection semantics, including under v1 replay (§12).

For a V0.2 batch: decode strictly; build a private proposed registry/timeline;
resolve existing entity references in input order; validate the final composed timeline;
compute whole-schedule reconciliation once; price obligations; then commit all
changes atomically. No intermediate roster swap may displace a patient. An
invalid item rejects the **entire batch**: that item gets its stable code, all
other items get `batch_rejected` with the failing index. Deterministic first
error order is schema, identity/role, dates, terms, composed roster, capacity.
No employee, contract, booking, posting, generation or event is committed on
failure. Action-attempt records are still appended, as in V0.1. References name
entities already committed before this batch; no implicit references to a hire
or contract created earlier in the same batch are supported. Hire includes its
complete initial roster; use its returned ID in subsequent batches.

Dates are practice-local `YYYY-MM-DD`; effects are 00:00 except immediate
acceptance obligations/reconciliation. Notice means calendar dates, not business
days. All requested effective dates must be within today's booking horizon
(default 120 days); subsequent recurring versions persist without a horizon
limit. `lastDay` is inclusive, employment ends at next midnight. Time passes
only after the batch. Same-timestamp events have already settled; commit queues
only events strictly later than now (`internal/sim/engine.go:229`).

Canonicalize weekdays, skills, covers and windows before semantic comparison.
An identical existing instruction is `ok:true, code:unchanged`; after basic
identity/shape checks it remains a no-op even when its original notice date is
now past. A no-op changes no operating state, costs, reservation, events,
generations or log; only the attempted-action record/digest grows. Empty batch
creates no record. A changed batch logs once per changed action and once per
actual lifecycle/displacement effect, settles once, and records assigned IDs.

### 6.4 Timeline composition, versions and extensions

For each employee/day: employment bounds → service-stage eligibility → latest
roster version effective on/before date → that rota week's pattern → approved
dated extension. Radiologists use immutable shifts plus allocation versions;
V0.1 extensions can extend on-site supervision, **not reading blocks**
(`internal/sim/actions.go:424`). This exception stays in the legacy action path;
V0.2 `set_roster` never edits a radiologist.

Each effective date is one version key. `op:set` replaces that key; insertion
before another version affects only the interval before the next version.
`op:remove` removes only that future key and reveals its predecessor until the
next version. Removal needs the same notice as replacement. Two writes to the
same employee/type/date in one batch reject as `invalid_parameters`; do not use
input order as accidental last-writer precedence. A version equal to its
predecessor need not be stored unless it replaces a differing scheduled version.
If it changes no effective interval it is `unchanged`. Past versions are immutable.

Validate both A/B weeks and every finite version/extension/employment/training
boundary, plus adjacent days, not just the submitted template. Check shift and
cover references/skills, strictly interior ordered breaks, positive spans,
max shift, at most six workdays/week and ten hours' rest between **maximum
permitted release** and next start **[proposed]**. Pre-opening shifts remain
valid; patient slots must lie within opening hours. A shift may not cross
midnight; absent an explicit narrower cap, start+maxShift must be before
24:00 as today. With `maxWorkUntil`, cap = min(start+maxShift, explicit cap),
end ≤ cap < midnight. Runtime admission/hold-over must use that same cap.

Require at least 30 unpaid break minutes for a shift longer than six hours,
with no planned uninterrupted segment longer than six hours **[proposed]**.
Existing unedited legacy rosters remain valid; changes validate the changed
employee's composed schedule. Report entitlement/taken/shortfall even for legacy
rosters rather than rewriting them. Rest checks use caps conservatively and
cannot assume a task's latent finish time.

Extensions are durations in the current engine (`internal/sim/actions.go:110`).
Preserve an existing approval only if the new composed shift still exists,
provides its declared coverage and satisfies cap, rest and break rules. A roster
change that conflicts **rejects**, with extension ID/date; do not silently add
the duration to an incompatible shift. Termination alone voids all approvals
on/after employment end, lists them in preview, and retains cancellation history;
there is no payroll charge for unworked extension hours beyond the guarantee.
Extensions affecting V0.2-created employees or changed future plans validate
against approved versions and employment/training bounds. Untouched legacy
employee/day contexts retain V0.1 extension validation/results; do not apply new
rest/break rules retroactively to a historical v1 action record. Future versions at/after termination are voided, not secretly resurrected.

### 6.5 Previews and result contract

A preview returns `asOf`, action results (without allocated IDs), final planned
coverage, projected onboarding completion, affected dates, surviving booking IDs,
potential displacement IDs/causes, extension/version conflicts, and two separate
money tables:

* **Guaranteed obligations:** recruiting fee, contractual weekly base/benefits,
  minimum notice and severance, committed salary through an approved change,
  contract retainer through earliest exit, already accrued liabilities and
  settlement dates. Show deltas and totals; active indefinite employment also
  shows weekly continuing cost and earliest lawful exit cost.
* **Forecast incremental costs:** rostered worked base above the guarantee,
  weekly overtime, likely hold-over, outsourced read counts/fees, collection
  timing ranges and possible lost/deferred contribution. Use published duration
  quantiles and trailing observed demand (28 days; state available history).
  Label the horizon and assumptions; never call these guaranteed amounts.

Known bookings can support deterministic capacity previews; patient tolerance,
cancellation/no-show behavior, future demand, actual read duration and payer
outcomes cannot. A potential displacement set is exact under the submitted
coverage, but rescheduling acceptance and downstream freed capacity are unknown.

## 7. Whole-schedule reconciliation and patient displacement

### 7.1 Scope and deterministic survivor selection

`sharedFree` is an **insertion predicate** (`internal/sim/demand.go:273`), not a
validator for occupied slots. It assumes existing reservations are valid, can
skip unstaffed pieces, and can count an occupied proposed slot twice. V0.2 must
not call it independently on every occupied slot and call that reconciliation.

For every changed future day, collect the **entire affected booking set** across
all rooms, including transitive shared-technologist conflicts and all already
materialized days through the horizon. Rebuild coverage from final batch state.
Later unmaterialized days use the same plan accessor. There are no booked days
outside the materialized horizon; recall orders retain their original wanted
dates. Earlier days and patients already on site are untouched by these
future-effective actions.

1. Build a temporary reservation set with no future bookings. Recompute each
   slot's full-interval bookability, supervision and staff sets from coverage.
2. Survivor order: priority (urgent, soon, routine), original appointment start,
   original confirmation time, appointment ID. This is deliberately separate
   from the order used to offer replacement dates. No payer or contribution key.
3. For each candidate, test category, room/time, complete coverage, contrast
   supervision and insertion feasibility **against only retained reservations**.
   Search alternate qualified technician assignments for the entire conflict
   cluster before dropping this booking. At each constant-coverage segment use
   distinct qualified techs, deterministic ascending staff order and backtracking;
   allow the same segment-boundary handover as current booking. Do not commit a
   greedy technician choice that unnecessarily evicts another booking.
4. Retain feasible candidates at the original time; staffing reassignment does
   not change appointment or attempt identity. Candidates that cannot retain
   time become potential displacements. Recheck the final retained set as a
   whole, with zero-staff stretches forbidden even for non-shared slots. The same
   validator must cover initial future-plan construction and replacement
   insertions; it cannot be limited to the final newly inserted appointment.
5. Release **all** displaced reservations before offering any replacement.
   Reoffer using §7.2, checking each accepted insertion and the complete final
   reservation set. This algorithm is deterministic, not a maximum-throughput
   optimizer; survivor priority is a published allocation rule.

Desk removal changes registration waits/self-registration, not room-slot
bookability: current centralized booking consumes no desk labor. Preview that
service risk; do not invent a desk-based automatic cancellation rule.
Allocation-only changes do not alter on-site contrast supervision and therefore
do not cause imaging-booking reconciliation.

### 7.2 Displacement state, offers and identity

Thus a future patient is not unexpectedly moved to an earlier appointment.

First attempt reassignment at the original time (§7.1); only then offer a date.

Use the existing patient preferred-window/any-window choice and tolerance

| Population | Identity / use |
|---|---|
| New demand | Unique referral and walk-in origins received in `(t0,T90]`, including refusals, booking loss and abandonment; retain modality, priority, source and payer strata |
| Inherited patient obligations | Orders/bookings outstanding at t0, separately tracked through completion, loss or tail; displacements never divide by only new receipts |
| Exam/report cohorts | Exams completed in-window, plus unread studies inherited at t0; report completion, overdue exposure and censoring stay attached to those cohorts |
| Attempt/event history | All cancellations, replacements, failed offers and repeated displacements; event counts cannot substitute for unique-order outcomes |

For throughput/access gates, a due order has original wanted/first promised
service time ≤ the evaluation boundary, never its delayed replacement date.
Apply the same rule to the paired no-action baseline. Show not-yet-due demand
separately; moving a promise cannot remove an order from the due denominator.
Preserve walk-in origin after a booking conversion. Do not reuse legacy
`LostRate` as a cohort success measure (`internal/sim/metrics.go:309`).

### 13.2 Visible metric vector

No weighted score. Display the vector, each denominator, the baseline difference,
gate status and uncertainty. Raw values remain visible on **every** failed gate.

| Dimension | Required fields |
|---|---|
| Operating performance | Raw revenue, expenses by account, operating profit/margin; contribution by modality; days 31–90; zero revenue gives undefined margin, not success |
| Cash / financing | T90 cash, minimum cash/date, A/R and A/P, accrued payroll, provisional guarantee, severance payable, due/past-due amounts, earned unposted wages, minimum future obligations and funding gap; inherited vs new attribution |
| Completed service | Exams and reports, separately; served fraction of all due demand including refused patients; by modality/priority and source/payer strata; inherited backlog resolution |
| Lost demand | Unique referral loss and walk-in abandonment/conversion; patient cancellation/no-show separate from practice displacement; unresolved orders and repeated displacement events |
| Access | Original wanted-to-first-booked, wanted-to-current-booked and wanted-to-actual-service distributions; p50/p90/max and unfinished current age, including inherited patients and displacement delay |
| Urgent TAT | Breach fraction/count, elapsed overdue study-hours, p90/max completed TAT, unfinished/overdue counts and oldest age; soon shown separately |
| Routine TAT | Completed p50/p90/max, unfinished count/age, overdue fraction and exposure, report-completion fraction; same fields for soon and each modality |
| Workforce obligations | Contracted, rostered and worked hours, training hours, scheduled-end overrun, weekly premium hours, break entitlement/taken/shortfall, maximum consecutive duty/rest deficits, payroll/notice/severance/retainer schedule |
| Reading mix | Employed/remote counts, costs, remote share, handoff and coverage-gap exposure, outcome tails by reader kind; no assumed quality equivalence beyond modeled measures |

Payer strata use only payer identities already disclosed through public
history; undisclosed payer stays in an explicit unknown group. Evaluation must
not draw or expose a refused patient's latent payer just to classify that loss.
Modality, source and priority coverage still uses the whole original cohort.

An unfinished study is right-censored at current age, not assigned a fictitious
completed TAT. Publish completed distributions **alongside** censored counts,
ages and overdue exposure; never let completed-only medians pass a gate by
starving the slowest cases. Runtime and calibration currently use different
TAT summaries (`internal/sim/metrics.go:441`, `internal/sim/calib_test.go:389`);
the new cohort report must make them comparable without rewriting old metrics.

Break entitlement is at least 30 minutes for a shift over six hours and no
uninterrupted stretch over six hours under the proposed roster rules. Count
missing **entitlement**, including unscheduled breaks, separately from existing
scheduled-break skips. Weekly premium hours start above 40 actual paid hours;
roster overruns measure a different quantity. Longer rostered shifts cannot
hide overtime or break deprivation. Genuine part-time hires can reduce premium
pay; that is an economic choice with hiring/training/guarantee costs, not itself
an exploit.

### 13.3 Baseline-relative minimum service gates

Let `B[m,s,h]` be the measured no-action baseline metric for scenario, stratum s
and horizon/cohort h. Step 0 supplies paired seeds and its 20-seed distribution;
the chosen scenario's no-action baseline is measured again with final contracts
and costs before release. Demand-weighted candidates use the matched-demand
reference specified in §4; a scenario cannot select a worse reference after
seeing an action plan. Store baseline version/hash and all thresholds in the
evaluation spec. No numeric service threshold is asserted by this draft.

For beneficial metrics use `L(m,s,h) = B[m,s,h] × (1 − DELTA_m_s_h)` and require
`X[m,s,h] ≥ L`. For harm metrics use
`U(m,s,h) = B[m,s,h] + DELTA_m_s_h × max(B[m,s,h], EPS_m_s_h)` and require `X ≤ U`.
`DELTA_* = PENDING_BASELINE_CALIBRATION` is a named nonnegative tolerance,
chosen from baseline variability and an achievable service envelope **before**
management-plan selection. `EPS_*` is a published measurement unit (one event
per fixed cohort for rates, one minute for delay, one study-hour for exposure),
not a hidden favorable target. An empty baseline stratum cannot automatically
pass: pool over the published seed set or mark it insufficient evidence and
require a declared aggregate/reference policy. No division by zero.

| Gate | Test and named threshold placeholders |
|---|---|
| Service volume and case mix | Due-demand exam served fraction and absolute completed exams ≥ L with `DELTA_EXAM_*`; same for every sufficiently supported modality/priority/source/payer stratum; fixed demand denominators prevent high-margin-only selection |
| Reporting | Due-report completion fraction ≥ L (`DELTA_REPORT_*`); unfinished overdue fraction and overdue study-hours ≤ U (`DELTA_UNREAD_*`); include inherited backlog and exams awaiting finalization at T90 and tailEnd |
| Demand retention | Unique referral loss, walk-in abandonment and combined practice-caused loss rates ≤ U (`DELTA_LOSS_*`); denominator is the original receipt cohort, with inherited losses in their own cohort |
| Displacement and access | Unique displaced-order fraction, repeat-displacement count, actual/current access p90, overdue waiting exposure and unresolved due fraction ≤ U (`DELTA_DISPLACE_*`, `DELTA_ACCESS_*`); original promise/tolerance anchors remain fixed |
| Urgent / soon service | Breach fraction, overdue exposure, unfinished oldest age and completed p90 ≤ U (`DELTA_URGENT_*`, `DELTA_SOON_*`); a zero observed baseline uses EPS and the calibrated tolerance, not arbitrary zero-breach perfection |
| Routine service | Routine completed p90, overdue exposure and unfinished oldest age ≤ U (`DELTA_ROUTINE_*`), plus reporting gate; median alone never decides eligibility |
| Sustainable coverage | Qualified jointly feasible booked/template capacity-hours and registration-capable duty hours ≥ L (`DELTA_COVERAGE_*`), by modality/week type through the tail; use baseline slot/open-hour denominators, not a reduced action-created denominator |
| Workforce | Weekly premium hours, break-entitlement shortfall and rest deficits ≤ U (`DELTA_WORKFORCE_*`); separately enforce legal-in-model roster/rest constraints and binding payment terms |
| Funding and obligations | Funding shortfall, late obligation amount/days and minimum cash drawdown no worse than U (`DELTA_FUNDING_*`) relative to the measured funded reference; no unauthorized borrowing, deleted liability or intentionally unpaid due bill can qualify |

A plan is service-eligible only when **all** applicable gates pass over T90 and
the defined tail checks. Aggregate gate thresholds are frozen from the measured
baseline distribution; report per-seed violations and paired confidence
intervals as well as aggregate status. Do not select a seed or hide rare failures
behind the mean. Before code, adopt `MAX_GATE_FAILURE_SEED_FRACTION` and
`MIN_EVIDENCE_COHORT_SIZE` from the sweep/owner risk decision (§17).

Financial success requires positive operating profit, acceptable cash/funding
and service eligibility, not merely a favorable margin among a few retained
patients. Raw P&L stays posting-based. A separate endpoint obligation bridge
adds earned-but-unposted wages and claim-committed remote fees, then lists
recognized liabilities and future unavoidable wages/severance/retainers without
double counting. Cash required to settle these obligations is compared with
available cash and explicitly authorized financing; latent receipts are not
certain funding. There is no new financing action in V0.2. Show a funding need
rather than invent a credit facility. A positive raw margin with unpaid or
unfunded obligations is labelled financially incomplete, not a management win.

Outsourcing passes the same report/access/quality and funding gates, plus actual
eligibility/coverage/concurrency constraints. More outsourcing is not inherently
bad: if it is cheaper and meets measured service obligations, the vector should
show that result. Excessive outsourcing that leaves paid in-house capacity idle,
misses urgent windows or creates unfunded retainers appears in cost, utilization,
TAT and commitment metrics; no fabricated price floor is needed.
