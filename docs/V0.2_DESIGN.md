# DreamTeam V0.2 — Design: a sustainable practice, staffing and reading coverage

Status: **draft 1, reviewed — NO GO for implementation as written**
(2026-10-14; `docs/reviews/v0.2-design-astra-review.md`: direction
endorsed, 18 findings to resolve in draft 2 after the owner's decisions). Nothing here is implemented. It
proposes the V0.2 scope, a second scenario, four management actions and the
engine changes they need, and lists the decisions the owner must make
before implementation starts (§12).

All numbers are **[illustrative]** starting points unless marked as
measured. Measured figures come from the V0.1 calibration
(`docs/calibration/step-18-report.md`, `docs/calibration/results/`).

---

## 1. Goals

V0.1 showed the practice's real problems and gave the player too few levers
to address them. V0.2 adds the levers the measurements point to, so the
player faces meaningful trade-offs among **profitability, patient access
and reporting quality**:

1. **A financially sustainable scenario** (`sustainable`): a better-staffed
   practice with more demand that can reach a +6% to +12% operating margin
   when run well, alongside the struggling default.
2. **Hiring and termination** of technologists and front-desk staff, with
   recruiting lead time, onboarding, notice and severance.
3. **Roster management**: change who works when, from a future effective
   date, with a coverage and cost preview.
4. **Radiologist reading coverage**: change employed radiologists' site
   allocation, and contract teleradiology for evenings, nights and weekends.

### 1.1 Foundations preserved (non-negotiable)

* **Determinism.** Keyed SHA-256 draws by origin; no wall clock; same
  scenario + seed + actions ⇒ same digests on every platform. New hidden
  draws are keyed by stable identities (§9.2).
* **Replay is authoritative.** Sessions remain scenario + seed + action
  batches; new action types are recorded and replayed like the V0.1 ones.
* **No-action neutrality.** With no V0.2 actions, the V0.1 scenarios'
  outcomes are unchanged: all four V0.1 golden references stay
  outcome-identical. Any engine change that alters them is a defect.
* **Golden updates are explicit** and limited to references whose inputs
  or outcomes actually change.
* **No artificial revenue.** Reimbursement stays as documented; quality
  does not earn money directly in V0.2 (§7.3).
* **The urgent-first worklist rule** and every V0.1 player-facing rule
  stay.
* **The struggling default stays the default** and is not recalibrated.

### 1.2 Non-goals for V0.2

Equipment purchase, new rooms, extended opening hours or Sunday opening,
referral dynamics, competition, payer contracting, raises, turnover and
fatigue, staff absences and other disruptions, template editing,
overbooking, the agent interface, and mouse support. These remain in later
milestones (§2).

## 3. What V0.1 measured (the problem)

Nine seeds, established practice (measured):

* Demand ≈1,650 orders and walk-ins a month; exams ≈1,300–1,380; margin
  −12.5% over 30 days, −14.0% over 90.
* Imaging is the constraint: XR/CT technologists 76–86% busy, US and MR
  each one technologist with fixed templates; US loses the most bookings
  (lead p50 ≈19 days, of which ≈10 requested and ≈3 capacity delay).
* Reading is over-provisioned (63–76% of site blocks used); the takeover
  worklist is ≈0.
* Three more technologists recover 65% of lost referral bookings but add
  only 7% exams and drop the margin to −18.6%: **at documented demand,
  extra staff cannot pay for themselves.**
* Urgent TAT: ≈¾ of urgent breaches are studies acquired after the last
  reading block (weekday evenings, Saturday afternoons), read next morning
  or Monday; the max is 31–41 h under every worklist policy.

### 3.1 Unit economics (derived from the documented inputs)

Approximate contribution per completed exam = expected net revenue −
variable cost (supplies/contrast, $3 IT, 5% billing fee):

| Modality | Net revenue per report (measured) | Contribution ≈ |
|---|---|---|
| XR | $51 | ≈ $40 |
| US | $174 | ≈ $155 |
| CT | $234 | ≈ $190 (supplies $15, or $50 with contrast; about half contrast) |
| MR | $321 | ≈ $260 (supplies $20, or $80 with contrast; ≈40% contrast) |

A full-time hourly technologist costs ≈ $36–45/h × 173 h × 1.25 benefits
≈ **$7.8k–9.7k a month**. An evening US technologist therefore needs ≈60
extra US exams a month to pay for themselves, an MR technologist ≈37 MR
exams, an XR-only technologist ≈190 XR exams. A 10-point change in a
radiologist's site allocation is ≈ **$5k a month** ($480k × 0.10 / 12 ×
1.25).

These ratios are the game: staff pays when it unlocks **unmet demand in
high-contribution modalities**. In the default practice that unmet demand
is too small; in `sustainable` it exists.

## 4. Scenario: `sustainable`

### 4.1 Concept

The same DreamTeam Imaging Center a few years into a larger referral base:
more demand, especially in US, CT and MR, and a roster that already covers
more hours. It is **profitable when run well and loses money when run
badly**: the player's decisions decide the margin, not the scenario's
starting state alone.

It is a separate scenario file that `extends` `base/established.json`
(like `mini`), with its own fingerprint, warm-up and golden references. The
struggling `established` remains the default.

### 4.2 Levers (documented, defensible)

Only these may differ from the base; each is a scenario input, never a
hidden rule:

| Lever | Direction | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Referral volume | ×1.15–1.30 overall, weighted to US/CT/MR sources | a larger referral base; the only lever that adds revenue |
| Roster | + an XR/CT day technologist; + a US evening technologist (11:30–20:00); MR evening coverage (an MR technologist 11:30–20:00, or a second shift) | covers the hours §6.2 leaves dark |
| US templates | 45 → 40-minute slots for the US_45 category | 37-minute exams in 45-minute slots waste ≈18% of US time; a documented operating choice |
| Radiologist allocation | the measured stable allocation (≈55%/45%) | reading is over-provisioned at default volume; recalibrated to the new volume, not cut below stability |
| Opening cash | enough for ≈2 months of payroll | the larger practice carries larger receivables |

**Not levers:** reimbursement rates, payer mix and behaviour, cost rates
(pay, rent, leases, supplies), no-show and cancellation behaviour, patient
tolerance, read and exam durations.

### 4.3 Targets and calibration method

Calibrated with the V0.1 experiment harness (manifests, nine seeds, 30 and
90 days; acceptance at 20 seeds):

* **No actions, default policies:** T30 margin in **+2% to +8%** (a
  competent but not optimised start).
* **A good, hand-written action plan** (published with the scenario as an
  experiment manifest): T30 margin in **+6% to +12%**.
* **A bad plan** (e.g., hire two unneeded technologists, decline walk-ins):
  margin below the no-action band.
* Tue–Fri volumes, utilization and lead times inside the ROADMAP criteria
  12–14 bands, *including* a 5–20-study takeover worklist if the
  reallocated reading produces one naturally (it must not be forced).
* The calibration must be achievable by the levers in §4.2 alone. If it is
  not, the report says so and the owner decides; no other parameter moves.

The calibration report states every lever's final value with its
measured effect, as step 18 did.

## 5. Actions

All four follow the V0.1 action contract (SIMULATION_DESIGN §9.5): one
`Apply` interface, validated in `sim`, with a preview (`Validate`) that
changes nothing, a stable rejection code, a log entry, a record entry, and
replay. Effects start at stated simulation times, never retroactively.

### 5.1 `hire` — recruit a technologist or front-desk clerk

```json
{"type": "hire", "role": "technologist", "skills": ["US"],
 "rateCents": 4400, "roster": {"Tue": {"start": "11:30", "end": "20:00", "breaks": [["15:30","16:00"]], "assignment": "R2"}, …},
 "startDate": "2026-11-16"}
```

* **Roles:** technologist (skills ⊆ the practice's modalities), front
  desk. Radiologists are not hired (reading capacity is §5.4); managers
  are out of scope.
* **Pay:** `rateCents` must lie in the published band for the role and
  skills (e.g., US technologist $40–48/h) **[illustrative]**. Higher pay
  shortens recruiting (below).
* **Recruiting lead time:** the earliest `startDate` is today + the
  role's published lead time: technologist 28 days, desk 14 days; −7 days
  at the top of the pay band. Deterministic and visible: no hidden
  candidate draws in V0.2.
* **Onboarding:** the first 5 working days the new employee is paid and
  on the roster but **shadows**: they staff no room on their own and add
  no booking capacity; from day 6 they are a normal employee. (Productivity
  ramps beyond that are V0.5.)
* **One-time cost:** a recruiting fee (e.g., $3,000 technologist, $1,000
  desk) posted when the hire is accepted.
* **Bookability:** the new employee's rostered hours from the end of
  onboarding become bookable immediately (as V0.1 extensions do), so
  demand can fill them before the start date.
* **Validation codes:** `unknown_role`, `invalid_skills`, `rate_out_of_band`,
  `start_too_soon`, `invalid_roster` (shape, cap, rest, breaks, assignment
  skill), `headcount_limit` (a cap per role, e.g., 8 technologists:
  the building has four rooms).
* **Observable:** the employee appears immediately as "starting <date>"
  in the staff list and roster screens.

### 5.2 `terminate` — end an employment

```json
{"type": "terminate", "employee": "E09", "lastDay": "2026-12-04"}
```

* **Notice:** `lastDay` ≥ today + 14 days (paid notice; the employee
  works their roster until then).
* **Severance:** two weeks' pay, posted at the last day.
* **Booked appointments:** any booked appointment that the termination
  leaves without qualified coverage (by the V0.1 feasibility check) is
  shown in the preview and, if the action is accepted, **rebooked at the
  time of the action** through the normal offer (the patient may decline
  and be lost). Nothing is silently stranded.
* **Who:** hired employees and the scenario's technologists and desk
  staff; not radiologists (§5.4) or the ops manager.
* **Validation codes:** `unknown_employee`, `notice_too_short`,
  `not_terminable`, `already_terminating`.

### 5.3 `set_roster` — change an employee's weekly pattern

```json
{"type": "set_roster", "employee": "E06", "effective": "2026-11-02",
 "roster": {"Mon": null, "Tue": {"start": "11:30", "end": "20:00", …}, …, "Sat": {…}}}
```

* **Effective date:** ≥ today + 14 days (schedule notice), on a Monday.
  The pattern replaces the employee's roster from that date; earlier days
  keep the old one. Fortnight rota (week A/B) patterns are accepted in the
  same shape as the scenario file.
* **Rules (validated):** skills match assignments; shift ≤ the cap
  (`maxShiftHours`); ≥ 10 h rest between shifts; breaks inside shifts;
  ≤ 6 working days a week; the assignment room exists and is open.
* **Cost preview:** paid hours per week and the weekly overtime the new
  pattern would cause at the 40-hour threshold.
* **Booked appointments:** as for `terminate`: appointments the change
  leaves uncovered are listed in the preview and rebooked on acceptance.
* **Extensions:** V0.1 `extend_shift` approvals on affected days stay
  valid only if they still extend a shift; otherwise they are cancelled
  with a log entry (no cost).
* **Validation codes:** `unknown_employee`, `notice_too_short`,
  `not_monday`, `invalid_roster` (with the first violated rule).

### 5.4 Reading coverage

Two actions: one moves employed radiologists' time, one buys outside reads.

#### 5.4.1 `set_reading_allocation`

```json
{"type": "set_reading_allocation", "employee": "E07", "siteAllocationBP": 5500, "effective": "2026-11-02"}
```

* Changes an employed radiologist's site allocation (and so both their
  site reading blocks and the salary charged to the practice) from the
  effective date (≥ today + 14 days). Band 30%–100%.
* This lever can cut cost — and can make the reading queue unstable (the
  V0.1 experiment at 45%/38% gave a 22-hour routine median). The preview
  shows the site reading hours per week against the last four weeks'
  reading demand at published p50 read times.

#### 5.4.2 `contract_teleradiology`

```json
{"type": "contract_teleradiology", "windows": ["weeknights", "saturday", "sunday"],
 "scope": "urgent", "effective": "2026-11-02"}
```

* A teleradiology service reads studies **remotely** during contracted
  windows: `weeknights` 18:00–08:00, `saturday` 14:00–24:00, `sunday`
  all day **[illustrative]**.
* **Scope:** `urgent` (urgent studies only) or `all` (every study that
  reaches the worklist inside a window).
* **Pricing [illustrative]:** a monthly retainer per window (e.g.,
  $1,500 weeknights, $800 per weekend day) plus a per-read fee by
  modality (XR $12, US $30, CT $45, MR $60) with a night differential.
  No fee for studies the practice's own radiologists read first.
* **Mechanics:** the service is a reader with fixed concurrency (e.g.,
  two reads at a time), claiming by the worklist policy within its scope,
  only inside its windows. Read times are each case's own (the V0.1 keyed
  read-time draw), plus a fixed handoff delay (e.g., 10 minutes) for
  image transfer. It never supervises contrast (remote).
* **Cancellation:** with 30 days' notice; the retainer runs to the end.
* **Validation codes:** `invalid_window`, `invalid_scope`,
  `notice_too_short`, `already_contracted` (one contract at a time; change
  it by cancelling and re-contracting).
* **Revenue:** unchanged — the practice bills globally as now; the
  teleradiology fee is a cost (professional component purchased).

### 5.5 Actions summary

| Action | Earliest effect | Money | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| `hire` | today + 28 d (tech) | recruiting fee; payroll | access and volume vs labor cost |
| `terminate` | today + 14 d | severance; saves payroll | cost vs access; rebooked patients |
| `set_roster` | next Monday ≥ 14 d | overtime and shift cost | which hours and rooms are covered |
| `set_reading_allocation` | ≥ 14 d | ±$5k/month per 10 points | reading cost vs TAT stability |
| `contract_teleradiology` | ≥ 14 d | retainer + per read | urgent TAT vs cost |

The 14-day notice and 28-day recruiting periods make every staffing
decision a forecast: the player decides from observed trends (lead
times, lost bookings, backlog), never from hidden future demand.

## 6. Engine changes

### 6.1 Rosters with effective dates

Today the roster is a fixed weekly pattern per employee, materialized per
day into a `DayPlan` (§6.3, step 6). V0.2 makes it a **list of versions**,
each with an effective date; `planFor(day)` uses the version in force on
that day. Days already materialized (up to the booking horizon, 120 days)
are re-planned when a version changes, exactly as V0.1 re-plans a day for
an approved extension: slots' bookability, staff sets and shared flags are
recomputed (`coverSlot`), and the free-capacity check (§6.3) decides which
booked appointments are uncovered.

### 6.2 Employees added mid-run

Employees are currently fixed at scenario load (indexes into `emps`,
bit sets of up to 64 technologists in slot staff masks). Hired employees
are appended with the next ID (`E09`, …). Constraints:

* Staff bit masks stay `uint64`: the headcount limit keeps the total
  employee count ≤ 64 for the life of a session (terminated employees keep
  their index; IDs are never reused).
* Payroll, benefits accrual, metrics tallies (`emps` slices in snapshots),
  alert rules and the staff screens must accept employees that start and
  end mid-run; "on payroll" becomes date-bounded.
* The state digest covers the new employee records and roster versions.

### 6.3 Uncovered appointments

When a roster change, termination or allocation change takes effect on
materialized days, every booked appointment on those days is checked with
the V0.1 feasibility check (`sharedFree`, distinct qualified technologists
through the appointment's interval). Those that fail are listed in the
preview and, on acceptance, rebooked in deterministic order (day, start,
appointment ID) through `offer` with a recorded reason
(`rebooked: staffing change`). The patient's tolerance decides acceptance,
as for any rebooking; losses count in referral `lost` with that reason.

### 6.4 Teleradiology reader

A non-employee reader in the reading model: a capacity (concurrent reads),
windows, scope, a handoff delay and a price list. It claims through the
same worklist ordering (urgent first), after the practice's own
radiologists in the same transaction, so the site readers keep priority and
results stay deterministic. Its reads post a cost at finalization; its
reads count in TAT like any other. It is visible: the worklist shows
"Teleradiology" as the reader.

### 6.5 Finance

* New ledger accounts: recruiting (expense), severance (expense),
  teleradiology retainer and per-read fees (purchased professional
  services). They appear in the P&L, the cash bridge and the evaluation.
* Payroll for hired staff follows the existing biweekly cycle; partial
  periods are prorated by days employed.
* Allocation changes change the radiologist's daily salaried accrual from
  the effective date.

### 6.6 What must not change

`firstFit`, dispatch, the worklist policies, the closing rules, read-time
and exam-time draws, demand generation for the V0.1 scenarios and the
accounting of V0.1 events. The first engine step of V0.2 (dynamic rosters
with no versions used) must leave the four V0.1 golden references
outcome-identical — tested before anything else builds on it.

## 7. Trade-offs, metrics and how the player sees them

### 7.1 The decisions

* **Hire for access or not?** In `sustainable`, an evening US technologist
  recovers lost US bookings worth more than they cost; in `established`,
  they don't. The player has to read lead times and lost bookings to know
  which practice they are in.
* **Reading cost vs TAT.** Cutting allocation saves money until the queue
  becomes unstable; teleradiology removes the overnight urgent tail at a
  price that service quality, not revenue, justifies.
* **Roster shape.** Covering MR evenings, Monday XR/CT and US mornings
  each cost the same hours but unlock different demand.

### 7.2 Service scorecard

The evaluation window gains a **service scorecard** reported next to the
financial results, from observed facts only:

* access: referral lead time p50/p90 by modality, lost bookings (rate and
  by reason), walk-in abandonment (cohort, as built in 18.1);
* reporting: urgent breaches (and how many waited overnight), urgent TAT
  p90, routine TAT median;
* workforce: overtime hours, skipped breaks.

### 7.3 Does quality earn anything?

In V0.2, **no**: reimbursement does not depend on TAT or access, and
referral volume does not respond to service (that is V0.5 referral
dynamics). The scorecard makes the trade-off visible and scoreable (for
the benchmark and scenario objectives) without inventing revenue. This is
an explicit owner decision (§12, Q3); the alternative is to bring a minimal,
documented referral response forward.

## 8. Exploits and guards

| Exploit | Guard |
|---|---|
| Hire before a known busy period, fire after | 28-day recruiting, 5-day shadowing, 14-day notice, severance, recruiting fee: a hire–fire cycle costs > a month of wages |
| Fire everyone to cut costs at the end of an evaluation window | notice and severance post inside the window; uncovered appointments are rebooked and lost demand counts; the scorecard shows the access collapse |
| Roster thrash to chase daily demand | 14-day notice, Monday effective dates; demand is hidden, only patterns are public |
| Cut radiologist allocation and buy teleradiology `all` cheaply | per-read fees exceed the salaried cost per read at normal volume [checked in calibration]; preview shows both |
| Teleradiology for urgent only to game the urgent metric while routine rots | the scorecard reports routine TAT next to urgent |
| Strand booked patients by changing rosters | every uncovered appointment is rebooked at the action and counted |
| Hire above 64 employees to break bit masks | headcount limit per role, total ≤ 64 per session |
| Use extensions to avoid hiring | allowed — V0.1 extensions are a legitimate, costlier short-term tool; overtime is ×1.5 |

Each guard gets a regression test that tries the exploit.

## 9. Determinism, replay and golden references

### 9.1 Records

New action types are appended to the session's action batches like V0.1
actions. A V0.1 engine refuses a V0.2 record cleanly: the record decoder
disallows unknown fields, so the new action fields fail decoding (and an
unknown type alone would be rejected as `unknown_action` and fail replay
on the recorded results). The schema can therefore stay
`dreamteam.session/v1`, with the engine version check reporting the
mismatch; the owner may prefer an explicit `v2` (§12). A V0.1 session
replays unchanged on the V0.2 engine (golden test).

### 9.2 Keyed draws

V0.2 adds no hidden randomness except what already exists per order and
study (patient behaviour, read times). Recruiting and onboarding are
deterministic. If a later version adds hidden hiring outcomes, they are
keyed by the action's record identity (batch and index), never by
processing order.

### 9.3 Golden references

* The four V0.1 references: **outcome-identical**, not regenerated
  (outcomes unchanged; the engine name in them is reported as before).
* New: `sustainable-seed1-30d.json` (no actions), `sustainable-seed1-30d-plan.json`
  (the published good plan), and an `established` run exercising every V0.2
  action, including rejections.

### 9.4 Cross-platform

The CI matrix and byte-identical comparison extend to the new references.

## 10. Testing and acceptance (V0.2)

1. V0.1 golden references outcome-identical after every V0.2 engine step.
2. Each action: validation table (every rejection code), preview = apply
   (no state change on preview or rejection), not retroactive, replay
   equivalence, chunking invariance across its effective date.
3. Rosters: re-planning a materialized day equals building it fresh with
   the new version (property test over random roster changes).
4. Uncovered appointments: none remains booked without a feasible
   technologist assignment after any accepted staffing action (invariant).
5. Teleradiology: claims only in windows and scope; never supervises;
   reads equal to the case's own read time plus handoff; costs post at
   finalization; overnight urgent breaches fall (experiment).
6. Exploit tests for every row of §8.
7. `sustainable` calibration bands (§4.3) at 20 seeds; the good plan beats
   no actions on margin in ≥ 80% of seeds and the bad plan loses to it.
8. Invariants over 50 seeds × 90 days with random valid V0.2 actions
   (acceptance tag).
9. TUI: hiring, roster and reading screens at 80×24 and 140×40; snapshots;
   TUI-to-headless replay with the new actions.

## 12. Decisions for the owner

1. **Roadmap re-scope** (§2): V0.2 = practice design; operations control
   moves to V0.3, finance to V0.4, growth to V0.5 without hiring.
2. **`sustainable` levers** (§4.2): is a referral volume increase
   (×1.15–1.30) acceptable as the scenario's premise? Without it, no
   staffing makes the practice profitable at documented rates.
3. **Quality and money** (§7.3): keep quality revenue-neutral in V0.2
   (scorecard only), or bring forward a minimal referral response to
   access and TAT?
4. **Hiring model** (§5.1): deterministic published lead times and
   shadowing (proposed), or a hidden candidate pool with keyed outcomes?
5. **Radiologists**: allocation changes and teleradiology only (proposed),
   or also hiring radiologists?
6. **Notice periods**: 14 days for rosters and terminations, 28 for
   recruiting, 30 for teleradiology cancellation — acceptable defaults?
7. **Teleradiology pricing** (§5.4.2): adopt the illustrative price list
   for calibration, or supply reference prices?
8. **Scorecard weighting**: report only (proposed), or define a weighted
   score now for scenario objectives and the benchmark?
9. **Session schema**: keep `dreamteam.session/v1` with new action types
   (proposed), or bump to `v2`?
