## 1. Owner decisions and goals

### 1.1 Binding owner decisions (quoted)

> 1. Contracted hours: YES, mandatory for hourly employees. Each hourly employee
> has a contractual weekly-hours commitment alongside the actual roster.
> - Contracted hours determine minimum compensation and notice/severance
>   obligations.
> - Rostered hours determine planned coverage.
> - Worked hours determine actual utilization and overtime calculations.
> A player cannot reduce a 40-hour employee to zero scheduled hours and
> eliminate payroll costs without formally terminating. Use guaranteed weekly
> base pay, with actual worked hours and applicable overtime accounted for
> without double-paying the same hours. Notice pay and severance use the
> contracted weekly baseline. Contract changes are NOT a separate V0.2 player
> action: hire establishes the contract, terminate ends it, set_roster cannot
> silently change it.

> 2. Evaluation: T90 is the primary management evaluation. T30 is early
> operational and financial feedback; T180 is optional longer-term validation.
> Use a transparent metric vector, not a single weighted score: operating
> margin, cash/financing position, completed exams, lost demand, access
> delays, urgent TAT breaches, routine TAT, workforce obligations. Set minimum
> service gates so a player cannot look successful by eliminating staff,
> refusing patients or postponing reports. Gates are grounded in the
> scenario's measured achievable baseline, not arbitrary perfection.

> 3. Radiologist roster edits: EXCLUDED from V0.2. set_roster applies only to
> hourly technologists and front-desk employees. Radiologist coverage is
> managed by set_reading_allocation and contract_teleradiology, with clearly
> defined costs (do not mix salaried allocation with hourly shift coverage).

> 4. No-action neutrality: for existing V0.1 scenarios with no V0.2 actions,
> patient flow, reporting, accounting, logs and observable outcomes are

> digest and therefore the run digest; record and regenerate those

> golden references as historical compatibility fixtures rather than

> 5. Session schema: new sessions use v2 (new action types and lifecycle

> existing records under documented compatibility rules. Do not promise that
> old state digests match a new engine's state schema; report outcome
> compatibility separately.

> 6. Feasibility sweep: mandatory Step 0 and a HARD GATE before any action code.
> Static scenario overlays representing the eventual steady-state effects of
> the actions. If a profitable practice is not possible with only the
> documented staffing, demand, template and reading-allocation levers, stop
> and revisit the design.

### 1.2 Goals and preserved foundations

V0.2 supplies five management actions: `hire`, `terminate`, `set_roster`,
`set_reading_allocation`, `contract_teleradiology`. It adds an employee lifecycle,
a separate candidate `sustainable` scenario, and a T90 evaluation vector with
service eligibility gates. Profitability and marginal hiring opportunities are
hypotheses until measured; neither +6–12% margin nor a particular backlog is a
release requirement.

Preserve integer time/money, practice-local time without DST, origin-keyed
randomness, timestamp transactions, authoritative replay, immutable scenario
inputs, and the observation boundary. Preserve existing V0.1 action semantics
in the legacy path, including action messages and serialization. Keep
`established` the struggling default and `mini` a compatibility fixture.
Revenue stays global billing at report final; service quality does not directly
change reimbursement or referrals in V0.2. Preserve existing worklist ordering,
including urgent protection and the default policy's promotion of overdue work
(`internal/sim/reading.go:108`); do not silently redefine that policy.

Radiologist hiring, salary negotiation, employee contract amendments, absences,
fatigue, equipment expansion, new opening hours, virtual supervision, template
editing by players, and discretionary patient rescheduling are outside V0.2.
Practice-caused displacement is required now because staffing actions need it.
Static template changes in Step 0 are scenario authoring, not a sixth action.

## 3. Measured problem and corrected unit economics

Nine-seed V0.1 measurements: 30-day margin −12.5%, 90-day margin −14.0%; about
1,314–1,381 exams per 30.4-day month from about 1,650 monthly orders and
walk-ins. Adding three full-time technologists reduces booking losses but
produces a 90-day margin of −18.6%. The three-tech T30 result is $223.2k revenue,
$260.4k expense and −$37.2k profit. Source: `docs/calibration/step-18-report.md`
and `docs/calibration/results/summary.md`. The latter reports exams per **30**
days; do not mix that normalization with the report's 30.4-day month.

The payer outcomes are mutually exclusive, with expected collection fraction
`(1 − denied − short) + short × meanShort + denied × recovered`
(`internal/sim/finance.go:60`). The input mix gives net/base = **1.13305**.
The following are derived **referral-input mix estimates**, not measurements
of the completed case mix:

| US | $176.38 | $8.00 | $156.56 |
| CT | $235.24 | $31.67 | $188.81 |
| MR | $321.53 | $31.89 | $270.57 |

Inputs: `scenarios/base/established.json:105`, `scenarios/base/established.json:201`,
`scenarios/base/established.json:260`. Walk-ins, losses, cancellations and
selective completion change realized mix. Billing fees accrue on collections
(`internal/sim/finance.go:527`), so this lifetime arithmetic is not a T30 P&L

At 173 paid hours/month and 25% benefits: XR $36/h costs $7,785 (about 184 extra
completed-and-reported XR exams); US $44/h costs $9,515 (61 US); MR $45/h costs
$9,731 (36 MR). Recruitment, training, overtime and reading can raise these
break-even quantities. Three additional $38/$44/$45 technicians cost about
$27,464/month. Fixed monthly expenses are $88,500. Cutting the combined
radiologist allocation by ten percentage points saves about $5,000/month:
`$480,000 × 0.10 / 12 × 1.25`.

### 3.1 Employed versus outsourced reading

At 60%/50%, employed radiologists cost approximately $55,000/month including
benefits, or $40 per report at 1,379 monthly reports. Draft 1's illustrative
outsourced mix averages approximately $29.31/read **before retainers and night
fees**. Thus the claimed outsourcing price guard was false. Salary is a fixed
site obligation while its allocation is in force; the immediate marginal wage
cost of another in-house read is zero. Allocation changes make a portion of
salary avoidable only after notice. Compare:

`avoidable allocation salary + benefits` versus
`incremental retainer + fees for actual outsourced mix + night fees`.

Hold workload and service eligibility constant. Include retained salary,
capacity limits, handoff delay, and unfinished studies. Cheap outsourcing can
be a valid choice. There is no artificial price floor, revenue bonus, or
unmeasured clinical-quality penalty. §8 specifies operational quality limits;
a post-implementation allocation × contract experiment must test substitution.

**Part-time cells were blocked (resolved in Step 0C, §4.7).**
`internal/scenario/validate.go:317` required
`shift.start + maxShiftHours < 24:00`; with the 12-hour cap no shift could start
after 12:00, so 16:00–20:00 was rejected. Changing the global cap would

### 4.4 Gate decision rule and candidate handoff

A candidate is feasible only if, at twenty seeds:

1. Mean **and median** T90 operating profit are positive after any contractual
   guarantee adjustment; mean days-31–90 profit is also positive. Publish the
   fraction of individually profitable seeds and the lower confidence bound;
   do not translate a positive mean into a guarantee for each seed.
2. Its service vector passes the baseline-relative limits in §13 using a
   predeclared matched no-action reference (same demand and inputs, baseline
   roster/templates/allocation). Freeze the tolerances from baseline variability
   before selecting the winner. Reading backlog/overdue growth must pass too.
3. Cash/obligations reconcile; any financing requirement is explicit. A result
   requiring unmodelled free credit is not a sustainable release candidate.
4. A neighboring configuration leaves a measured useful management opportunity
   at takeover: improved service at a stated affordable cost, or increased
   profit without failing gates. Publish that comparison. Do not ship every
   profitable marginal hire already installed and advertise it as a decision.

Step 0 passes only with a complete, reproducible report satisfying these rules
within the listed levers. Failure to find profitable service-eligible operation
is **NO GO: stop and revisit the premise**, not permission to change prices,
behavior or durations. Blocked cells or unfilled gates leave Step 0 pending.
The old 6–12% target is not imposed or presumed. Scenario-specific coverage and
service bands replace the old MR-dark-after-16:30 and forced-backlog targets.

After a pass, select a separate `sustainable` overlay with its own fingerprint,
warm-up and no-action baseline. Validate the actual five-action T90 plan later,
including recruitment and termination costs. T180 is optional validation;
T90 is mandatory. Static success alone does not establish interesting gameplay.

### 4.6 Step 0B: CT/XR booking losses and templates

Owner decision (2026-10-15): investigate why CT/XR referrals are lost while
their rooms are ≈40% used, and add static CT/XR templates as a permitted
lever. The fixes for the noon shift-start limit and teleradiology stay separate.
Method, tables and reproduction: `docs/calibration/feasibility/step0b/`.

**Diagnosis.** A passive booking-loss probe classifies every compatible slot
in each lost patient's acceptance window by the first FirstFit check it fails.
It assigns one primary cause by a documented precedence: shared technologist,
not rostered, no supervision, notice, template category, category full, no
template slot. It also records every cause present. Production change: one
function field on the state, excluded from the state digest like the
existing test hooks, and one nil-checked call in `offer`. Neutrality:
`TestLossProbeIsPassive` shows log, ledger, state and action digests
unchanged, and the 20-seed Step 0 candidate reproduces exactly with the probe
on.

At the Step 0 best candidate, 72% of lost CT/XR referrals (CT 135 of 197, XR
90 of 116 per 90 days) had a free, staffed, compatible slot in their window,
blocked because the **one technologist covering both rooms** was committed:
Riley all Monday and Tuesday–Friday mornings, Sam from 16:15. 92% of them
were urgent or soon patients (tolerance 0–10 days). The template category mix
explains ≈7% of CT losses. US losses are unstaffed US slots; MR losses (with
the evening technologist) are template mix and full templates.

**Template and coverage variants** (9 paired seeds, on the Step 0 best
candidate; 20 seeds for the best):

| Variant | T90 margin | Δ profit $k / 30 d | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 12-minute XR slots (`XR12`) | +0.5% (20 seeds; prior +0.4%) | +0.4; paired +$1.16k per 90 d, CI +0.36 to +1.96 | −45 referral losses, +39 walk-ins leaving; exams unchanged |

**Verdict: the gate still fails.** Templates are not the binding constraint

(more evening imaging means next-morning reports); and fixed costs with
salaried reading. The best permitted static configuration is now the Step 0
candidate with 12-minute XR slots: +0.5% mean T90 margin (−0.6 to +2.9), T90
profit +$3.9k (95% CI +$0.8k to +$7.0k), 14 of 20 seeds profitable. That is
consistently near zero, not materially profitable.

### 4.7 Step 0C: staffing architectures

Owner decision (option G, 2026-10-15): stop adjusting the struggling
practice's staffing and test explicitly different, fully costed coverage
architectures for a larger practice. Method, tables and reproduction:
`docs/calibration/feasibility/step0c/`.

**Prerequisite: late shifts.** A shift's cap (holdover included) is now the
earlier of start + `maxShiftHours` and accounting close, 23:59:59
(`sim.Policies.ShiftCap`); validation rejects a shift longer than the cap or
ending after accounting close, not one starting after noon. This replaces
the proposed `maxWorkUntil` field: no new scenario field, and it only
differs from the old rule for shifts that were invalid before. Regression
tests (written failing first): 16:00–20:00, 17:00–20:00, 11:30–20:00 and
19:00–23:59 valid; over-cap and past-midnight shifts rejected; late shifts
run with their room staffed and every engine invariant holding
(`TestShortLateShifts`, `TestShiftCap`, `TestShortLateShiftsRun`).
`TestRegress05MidnightBoundary` now runs its 12:00–23:45 fixture: the late
patient is rescheduled because the exam cannot finish before accounting
close, and nobody works past midnight. Goldens, replay and determinism tests
are unchanged; the 20-seed Step 0B candidate reproduces exactly.

**Where the conflicts were.** At the Step 0B best configuration, 62% of CT
shared-technologist losses fall on Monday (Riley staffs X-ray and CT alone,
mostly during the 12:00–16:00 contrast block); the rest fall on Tuesday–Friday
evenings (Sam alone) and, less, mornings.

**Architectures** (×1.30 demand, 12-minute XR slots; every new employee at
$38/h with 25% benefits; contracted hours = rostered paid hours): A shared
(control); B dedicated XR and CT day core with a part-time shared evening
(Sam 16:00–20:00, Monday evening newly staffed); C B plus part-time dedicated
evenings; D a part-time float in the measured conflict periods (Monday on CT,
Tue–Fri evenings on X-ray); E full-time pairs of B–D with the same coverage;
each with the evening MR technologist full-time (11:30–20:00) or part-time
(16:00–20:00); and the radiologists' site allocation at 60/50 (base),
62.5/52.5, 65/55 and 70/60.

**Findings** (9 paired seeds, 20 for the leaders):

* A part-time MR evening is worth +$4.9k a month with no volume change.
* Dedicated or float coverage removes 80–95% of the shared-technologist
  losses (+57 to +90 exams a month, −125 to −173 lost referrals in 90 days).
* Part-time beats full-time for the same qualified coverage in every pair,
  by $2.6–7.8k a month.
* The recovered volume overwhelms reading at the base allocation (urgent p90
  188 → 1,046 minutes, urgent breach rate 10% → 29%). Restoring it costs
  ≈$2.5k a month per 2.5 allocation points.

## 5. Staff registry, contracts and lifecycle

### 5.1 Authoritative registry and history

Current employees point into `cfg.Employees` (`internal/sim/world.go:83`);
plans read that array (`internal/sim/config.go:352`); metrics allocate and
subtract fixed employee slices (`internal/sim/metrics.go:75`,
`internal/sim/metrics.go:369`). V0.2 requires a registry **separate from immutable
scenario config**. Copy initial employee terms into registry records; mutable
roster/allocation/lifecycle versions belong to state and are digested. Do not
append to configuration or retain pointers into a reallocating slice.

Registry indices are append-only; departed employees and IDs remain. Initial
order is scenario order. New public IDs are the first unused `H00001`, …,
skipping every ID already present, including arbitrary scenario IDs. Allocate
only on accepted hire commit; preview/rejection allocates nothing. Store the
accepted batch/action identity and hire `requestId` on the employee. Repeating
that request with identical content is `unchanged`, even after departure;
different content is `request_conflict`.

Replace the fixed `uint64` staff mask (`internal/sim/demand.go:123`) with an
ordered growable bit set, retaining ascending registry-index iteration. Do not
reuse terminated bits or impose a lifetime recruitment limit. **[Proposed]**
concurrent caps are eight technologists and four desk employees; recruiting,
onboarding, active and notice all count, terminated do not. Existing scenarios
above a cap can load; additional hires reject until below it. Cap values are
scenario policy, with allocation/overflow bounds checked before append.
Remote contract IDs similarly use the first unused `T00001`, …, never recycled.
Request IDs are namespaced by action type. Per-entity plan revision numbers
increase only on committed changes; each version also records its accepting
batch/action ID. Version removals retain tombstones for audit and stale events.

Snapshots store registry length and cumulative values by stable index. An
employee absent from an older snapshot contributes zero; historical output lists
only employees disclosed by that period's end. Employment/roster metadata is
queried as of that end. Cached and uncached historical summaries must agree
before and after a hire. Keep departed employees in periods where they worked
or incurred obligations; current headcount never determines old array indexing.

### 5.2 Three distinct hours and compatibility migration

| Quantity | Definition | Drives |
|---|---|---|
| Contracted | Immutable weekly paid-hours commitment, `contractedWeeklyMinutes`; excludes overtime | Guaranteed base wages, notice baseline, severance |
| Rostered | Net planned shift time after unpaid breaks, including separately identified approved extensions | Future coverage and expected payroll above guarantee |
| Worked | Actual paid on-duty time, including idle availability, training and hold-over; excludes breaks taken | Utilization denominator, actual base pay, weekly overtime |

Busy task time is a subset of worked time; paid guarantee shortfall is **not**
worked time and creates no coverage or overtime. Training is worked paid time
but supplies no patient-service capacity. A zero-hour roster leaves the contract
and its payroll obligations intact. **[Proposed]** new hires and their later
regular rosters may not exceed contracted net minutes in either rota week;
approved dated extensions and actual hold-over are separately paid excess.
This prevents a nominal one-minute contract from carrying a recurring full-time
roster. Legacy unedited patterns retain their original A/B maxima; edited
patterns may not exceed the greater of the frozen original week total and C.
A part-time contract cannot become a recurring full-time roster without a new
employment contract, which V0.2 can establish only through termination/hire.

New hourly hires require 1–2,400 contracted minutes/week **[proposed]**. Every
initial hourly employee also has a contract. For a legacy scenario without the
field, infer the smaller net roster total of its two rota weeks, frozen at
load; do not assume every employee has 40 hours. In the supplied base, E01/E03/
E05 infer 40 h; E04 and E06 infer **39.5 h** (Saturday is 7.5 paid hours).
A legacy employee with no weekly duty may infer zero; an explicit V0.2 hire
cannot. Publish the inferred terms as metadata, never rewrite the scenario.

For partial weeks of legacy employment at the epoch, distribute that frozen
weekly guarantee across the original contract's rota weekdays in proportion to
net original roster minutes. These weights are immutable **contract accrual
weights**, not later roster edits. New contracts use equal 1/7 calendar-day
weights. Full weeks always guarantee C hours. This migration avoids inventing
Monday wages before a no-warm-up Tuesday starts and avoids extra no-action
postings for legacy patterns. §11 requires actual evidence of neutral postings,
not an exemption from guaranteed compensation after a roster edit.

### 5.3 Lifecycle and onboarding

`recruiting → onboarding → active → notice → terminated` is the display path.
Internally notice is an irrevocable termination overlay with `serviceStage`
(recruiting/onboarding/active) preserved until the end. This allows termination
of a pending hire or trainee without pretending they are productive.

| Stage | Contract/pay | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiting, accepted hire until startDate 00:00 | Recruiting fee recognized; future start/notice/severance commitment disclosed | Future active capacity only after computed training completion |
| Onboarding | Guarantee begins at startDate; actual paid training counts toward weekly worked hours | None: no independent registration, room staffing or contrast supervision |
| Active | Guaranteed wages plus worked excess/premium | Qualified roster coverage |
| Notice | Same terms until exclusive termination boundary | Same eligibility as underlying service stage; scheduled staff work normally |
| Terminated | No new service accrual; all earned pay, severance and unpaid bills survive | None; in-flight on-site work must already have ended by the preceding accounting close |

**[Proposed]** recruiting lead: technician 28 calendar days, desk 14; the premium
pay tier shortens it by seven days. No hidden candidate draws. Training requires
**40 attended paid hours** for a technician, **24** for desk, counted only in
rostered training intervals after breaks. No extension can accelerate training.
A provider-led orientation/preceptorship is included in the recruiting fee;
there is no modeled productive employee mentor or phantom shadow capacity.
Completion at a shift end enables independent work only from the next midnight.
Five tiny shifts cannot substitute for forty hours.

The deterministic training plan must complete within sixty calendar days of
start. Hire and subsequent roster edits validate the complete plan; projected
completion is visible and used in future bookability. A change that delays
completion reconciles every affected booking (§7). Invalid/incomplete training
plans reject; active employees may have an empty roster and still receive pay.
Initial scenario employees are active/trained from the epoch. Static sweep
hires likewise represent trained steady-state employees.

Termination before start is supported through `terminate`: lastDay must be at
least fourteen days after both the request date and contractual startDate.
The recruiting fee is non-refundable, guarantee starts on startDate even if the
roster is removed, and two weeks' severance remains due. There is no free
pending-hire cancellation path. After accepting termination, neither withdrawing
notice nor moving lastDay is supported in V0.2; identical repeats are no-ops.

### 5.4 Payroll arithmetic, notice and severance

Let C be the contract guarantee for a complete week, W actual worked hours,
r the hourly rate, and P = max(0, W − 40). The weekly economic wage obligation is:

`base = r × max(C, W)`

`premium = r × 0.5 × P`

`wages = base + premium`, with benefits `25% × wages` for the supplied scenario.

Use the scenario's weekly threshold/factor, not hardcoded values. The examples
use 40 hours and 1.5×. A premium is an additional 0.5× over base already paid;
guarantee and worked base never both pay the same hour. Partial employment
weeks prorate **only C** by frozen contractual weights on employed dates.
Never multiply actual worked wages by days employed a second time. Notice is
ordinary continued guarantee/worked compensation, not a second wage stream.
Severance = `2 × r × full contractual weekly hours`, plus benefits; no assumed
overtime, and no dependence on the last roster.

Preserve existing per-person/day actual-time postings and `HourlyPay` rounding
(`internal/sim/finance.go:362`). Maintain cumulative base-work cents B and earned
guarantee cents G for the week. At each day close, guarantee provision is
`Q = max(0, G − B)`. Post only `Q_new − Q_old`, signed; a decrease credits wage
expense and debits provisional guaranteed payroll as worked pay replaces the
guarantee. Hold this provision outside ordinary accrued payroll until weekly
finalization (§9); a Friday payday must not settle a still-reversible provision.
Use separate keys for these adjustments, never reverse ordinary worked pay.
G uses cumulative integer rounding; legacy day wages retain existing truncation.
For cumulative worked base B, use the sum of daily base-at-regular-rate cents,
excluding premium; keep premium cents separately under the existing `HourlyPay`
result so the total retains its daily rounding. Benefits use the scenario's
existing nearest-cent rule (`internal/sim/finance.go:355`).
At Sunday close or termination, reconcile the guarantee and benefits to cents,
then reset weekly counters Monday. Zero adjustments produce no ledger or log
entry. Actual wages and the net guarantee provision together implement the
formula, subject to the documented daily cent rounding. Benefits follow actual
postings at the current rule; provision benefits are a cumulative balance
adjustment so reversals cannot leave residual benefits.

| Worked example | Wage arithmetic | Benefits at 25% | Total |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| C=40 h, W=0, r=$40 | $1,600 guarantee, $0 worked/premium | $400 | $2,000/week |
| C=40, W=32 | $1,280 worked base + $320 guarantee shortfall | $400 | $2,000/week |
| C=40, W=45 | $1,800 base + $100 premium on five hours | $475 | $2,375/week |
| C=20, W=28 | $1,120 worked base, no guarantee top-up, no premium | $280 | $1,400/week |
| New C=40, employed Wed–Sun, W=16 | Guarantee 40×5/7 h: $1,142.85 at cumulative cent truncation; worked $640 replaces that amount, top-up $502.85 | $285.71 | $1,428.56 |
| C=40, two full notice weeks at W=0, then termination | $3,200 notice wages + $3,200 severance | $1,600 combined | $8,000; no third payment for the same notice hours |

Payroll liabilities settle on the existing anchored biweekly payday, including
after departure (`internal/sim/finance.go:282`). Severance is recognized when
termination is accepted (unconditional obligation) and paid on the first payday
after lastDay. It need not fall inside T90; §13 counts outstanding commitments.
Worked time this week, accrued payroll, and prior claims survive warm-up and
employee departure. Do not reset them at takeover or on a roster change.

### 5.5 Slice 1 as built (0.2.0-dev.1)

Slice 1 implements the contract and capacity foundation **without any new
player action**. What exists:

* **Contracts.** `sim.Contract` on every hourly `EmployeeConfig`: weekly
  contracted paid time, an `Inferred` flag and frozen accrual weights (the
  original net roster by rota week and weekday). A scenario may state
  `contractedWeeklyMinutes` (1–2,400, the hire band) for an hourly employee;
  otherwise the contract is inferred as the smaller net rota week, as §5.2
  proposes (E01/E03/E05 40 h, E04/E06 39.5 h). Stating it for a salaried
  employee is a validation error: salary is that employee's guarantee. The
  scenario file is never rewritten, and the canonical JSON (fingerprint) of a
  scenario without the field is unchanged.
* **Three hours.** Contracted (the contract), rostered (the plan) and worked
  (paid on-duty time, `weekPaid`, which alone drives weekly overtime) stay
  distinct. Guaranteed time is never worked time.
* **Guaranteed pay** (§5.4). Going home posts actual wages exactly as before
  and adds the day's worked base at the regular rate (premium excluded) to
  the week's counter B. At accounting close each hourly employee's guarantee
  G earns the day's share, `⌊r × C × w_d / (W_k × 3600)⌋` cents (w_d the day's
  weight, W_k its rota week's total; equal sevenths when W_k is 0), only on
  employed days. The provision moves to `max(0, G − B)` as a signed entry
  (`entGuarantee`: wages and benefits against accrued payroll); an unchanged
  shortfall posts nothing. Sunday's close finalizes the week (a log line when
  a top-up was owed) and resets the counters. Per-day truncation replaces the
  §11 cap: when C ≤ W_k a day's guarantee is at most the pay for its rostered
  time, which worked time (never below the roster in V0.1) already pays, so
  no legacy fixture can post a guarantee; a full week guarantees r × C less
  at most a cent per contract day.
* **Payday.** The current week's provision is accrued payroll that may still
  reverse, so a payday pays accrued payroll **less the open guarantee**; the
  finalized week is paid on the next payday after Sunday's close.
  *Revision of §9:* the provision stays inside `accrued_payroll`, with the
  per-employee subledger in state, instead of a separate liability account.
  A new account would add a row to the evaluation baseline's account list and
  so change `window.baselineDigest` in every historical reference; the
  payday exclusion gives the same cash behavior. Severance payable (Slice 2)
  faces the same choice.
* **Employment registry** (`employment.go`): one record per employee in
  state, in scenario order. Each holds a stable ID, lifecycle stage, the
  employed dates `[From, Until)`, the contract and the week's pay counters.
  Skills, rate and roster stay with the immutable scenario terms the record
  indexes. Stages recruiting/onboarding/notice/terminated are named but
  unreachable until Slice 2; every initial employee is active and trained
  from the epoch with no end. The registry is digested (state schema
  `dreamteam.state/v2`) and checked against the employees and the ledger
  every transaction.
* **Future obligations.** `severance(i)` = two contracted weeks at the base
  rate plus benefits, with no overtime and no dependence on the roster.
  Notice is ordinary continued pay (no separate stream). Observations show
  each employee's stage, contracted minutes, inferred flag, the week's open
  guarantee and the severance termination would owe.
* **Whole-schedule staffing check** (`World.CheckStaffing`, read-only, §7.1).
  For a hypothetical change (replacement weekly rosters from a future day), it
  rebuilds every materialized day's coverage and slot staffing. It then
  re-inserts the day's booked appointments in survivor order (priority, start,
  confirmation, ID), each against only the retained ones, using the booking
  rule's own predicates: `SlotBookable` for the whole slot and `sharedFree`
  for the whole shared-technologist cluster, with backtracking. It reports
  each appointment that cannot be retained, as `unstaffed`, `no_supervision`
  or `shared_capacity`. It changes nothing: no draw, log, cancellation or
  displacement. Approved extensions of unchanged employees stand.
  * Existing-booking consistency: 150 unchanged-roster checks (established,
    the sustainable start and mini; 10 seeds; 5 instants from takeover to
    day 89) found **no** inconsistent booking, so there is nothing to
    isolate.

Not yet built in Slice 1: hires and growable staff sets, displacement, the
actions, session v2, and severance/recruiting postings (all built in Slice

### 5.7 Slice 2 as built (0.2.0-dev.2): hire and terminate

The owner approved two actions for Slice 2, `hire` and `terminate`, and
nothing else (`set_roster`, `set_reading_allocation` and
`contract_teleradiology` come later). They are built to §5.1–§5.4,
§6.1–§6.3, §6.5, §7, §9 and §12 with the fixed offer table and lifecycle
rules of §5.3 and §6.3, which are now adopted terms. Deviations from the
text are listed at the end of this section. Without a V0.2 action, every
V0.1 outcome is unchanged (§11).

**Registry and identity.**
* An initial employee's terms are the scenario's, which stay immutable. A
  hire's terms (role, skills, rate, contract, roster) are frozen in its hire
  record, in state. Record *i* is employee *i*; records are only appended,
  and departed employees keep theirs.
* A hire's ID is the first unused `H00001`, `H00002`, … skipping every ID
  already present, scenario IDs included. IDs are allocated only when a
  batch commits; a preview or a refusal allocates nothing.
* Staffing sets are growable: the first 64 employees sit in one inline word
  (same order and arithmetic as the V0.1 mask), any more in further words.
  The scenario limit of 64 employees is raised to a 1,000-employee sanity
  bound. Per-employee metrics grow when a hire commits; an older snapshot
  reads a later employee as zero.

**Hire.** Validation stops at the first failure, in this order:

1. Shape (`invalid_schema`); a `requestId` repeated in the batch
   (`invalid_parameters`).
2. Identity: the same `requestId` already accepted with the same canonical
   content is `unchanged`, even after that employee has left; with other
   content it is `request_conflict`.
3. Role (`wrong_role`) and skills against the offer table (`invalid_skills`).
4. Start date: `invalid_date`, `past_date`, `beyond_horizon`.
5. Terms: the rate must be exactly the standard or the premium tier
   (`rate_out_of_band`); contract 1–2,400 minutes
   (`invalid_contract_hours`); the start no earlier than today plus the
   tier's recruiting lead (`lead_too_short`).
6. Roster (`invalid_roster`, with the field path). Each A/B weekday has one
   shift or none:
   * The shift lies inside the day and its cap.
   * Breaks lie strictly inside the shift, in order. A shift longer than
     6 h has at least 30 minutes of break, and no stretch longer than 6 h
     is worked without one.
   * A technologist is assigned to a room of a skill they hold; covers lie
     inside the shift, in qualified rooms, with no room twice at once. A
     front desk hire is assigned to the desk with no covers.
   * At most six working days in each rota week.
   * The net rostered time in each rota week is at most the contract.
   * At least 10 hours between one shift's cap and the next shift's start,
     over the 14-day cycle.
7. Training: the roster must give 40 attended hours (technologist) or 24
   (front desk), net of breaks, within 60 days of the start
   (`training_incomplete`).
8. Capacity: at most 8 technologists and 4 front desk staff at once.
   Recruiting, onboarding, active and notice all count
   (`headcount_limit`). A scenario already above a cap loads and runs; it
   cannot hire in that role until it is below the cap.

Committing a hire:
* Posts the recruiting fee (recruiting expense against A/P, paid with the
  bills on the 1st). It is never refunded.
* Opens the record in stage `recruiting`.
* Recomputes the planned coverage and slot staffing of every materialized
  day from the productive date. Adding capacity never invalidates an
  existing booking.

**Onboarding.**
* From the start date the contract guarantee accrues in equal sevenths of
  the week (§5.2), and training is paid as worked time.
* On a training day the employee works under a training view of the terms:
  role `trainee`, no skills. That view staffs no room, registers nobody,
  supervises nothing, is never held over, and accepts no extension (the
  extension is refused as `no_coverage`). The plan, the runtime and every
  booking predicate read the same view.
* Training is attended exactly as rostered. The projected completion
  therefore equals actual attendance, and an invariant checks that the
  required hours were attended before independent work begins.
* Independent work starts at the midnight after the completing shift (the
  productive date).

**Terminate.**
* Only hourly technologists and front desk staff can be terminated
  (`wrong_role`), and only before they have left (`not_employed`).
* The last day is at least 14 days after both today and the start date
  (`notice_too_short`), and within the horizon.
* The same last day again is `unchanged`; any other is
  `already_terminating`. Notice cannot be withdrawn.

Committing a termination:
* Employment ends at the midnight after the last day.
* Notice is the ordinary contract guarantee plus worked pay. A zero-hour
  roster pays the full guarantee (tested). The final partial week closes at
  the last day's accounting close.
* Severance is two contracted weeks at the base rate plus benefits. It is
  recognized at acceptance (severance expense against severance payable)
  and paid in cash on the first payday after the last day.
* Approved extensions on or after the end are voided, each with a log line.
* The bookings that lose coverage are reconciled once, after the whole
  batch has committed (see Displacement below).
* Terminating a pending hire keeps the fee, runs the contract from the
  start date and owes severance. There is no free cancellation.

**Lifecycle at midnight.** Before finance and the day's plan, in registry
order, each record moves to the day's stage and logs the change: start,
training complete, left. Notice is an overlay: a hire on notice still logs
its start and its training.

**Displacement: when and why.** Displacement happens at the decision
instant of a committed batch that takes coverage away. In Slice 2 that is a
termination, from its first day without the employee.

1. Every materialized day from the earliest such day is re-planned from the
   final registry: the whole batch, so a replacement hired in the same
   batch counts.
2. Each day's booked appointments are re-inserted in survivor order:
   priority, then start, then confirmation time, then appointment. Each is
   tested with `SlotBookable` and the full shared-technologist assignment
   search, against only the appointments already retained.
3. Every appointment that cannot be retained is displaced, with the first
   condition it failed: `unstaffed`, `no_supervision` or `shared_capacity`.
   All displaced reservations are released before any re-offer, and every
   day's coverage moves to the new plan.
4. Each displaced order is then offered a replacement, in this order:
   original start, priority, receipt, order, appointment. The replacement
   is a same-category slot no earlier than the original start, the
   original wanted date and the booking notice: the preferred-window slot
   over the horizon, else the first any-window slot.
5. The patient accepts if the replacement's day is within their tolerance
   of the tolerance anchor. The anchor is the earliest date of the current
   voluntary booking attempt, and a displacement never renews it.
   Otherwise the order is lost to displacement: `no_feasible_slot` or
   `replacement_declined`.

What a displacement keeps and records:
* The replacement keeps the behavioral attempt, the confirmation flag and
  the original no-show lead basis. The same no-show, cancellation, arrival
  and exam draws therefore apply at the new start, with no new random draw.
  A placement number counts the replacements.
* The displaced appointment is kept as history in its own state
  `displaced`. A lost order gets its own state, lost to displacement.
* Each event gets an immutable record: appointment, order, causing actions,
  cause, reason, time, replacement and resolution.
* Separate counters track events, orders, repeats, rebooked, no slot,
  declined and walk-in origin. They appear in the period metrics as
  `displacement` and stay out of the demand, loss, cancellation and
  no-show figures and denominators.
* Nothing is cancelled and no booking disappears.

Removing a front desk employee changes registration only and displaces no
booking (§7.1). Removing contrast supervision is impossible in Slice 2,
because radiologists cannot be terminated.

**Atomic batches.**
* A batch that contains `hire` or `terminate` holds only V0.2 actions;
  otherwise it is `mixed_batch`.
* Its items are validated in input order, each against the state plus the
  earlier items. One refusal refuses the batch: that item gets its own code
  and the others `batch_rejected`. No ID, posting, event or log line is
  committed, but every attempt is recorded.
* A valid batch commits in input order and then reconciles once.
* V0.1 batches keep their sequential semantics and exact messages.

**Finance.**
* Appended accounts: `recruiting` and `severance` (expenses) and
  `severance_payable` (liability). The V0.1 accounts keep their numbers. A
  V0.2 account is listed (trial balance, evaluation baseline) only once it
  has a balance. At the takeover it never has one, so the baseline digest
  is unchanged.
* Statements have `recruitingCents` and `severanceCents`. Labor includes
  severance; recruiting is its own line.
* The window's cash bridge adds the change in severance payable. The cash
  identity subtracts severance paid.
* The balance sheet, result balances and obligations show severance payable
  and severance due by payday.

**Preview.**
* `World.PreviewStaffing` (and `Session.PreviewStaffing`) validates as
  `Apply` would, then plans the proposed registry without committing.
* It returns each hire's offer, tier, rates, contract and rostered hours,
  start and productive dates, training, and the slots its capacity opens.
* It returns each termination's last day, notice, severance and payday,
  and the extensions it would void.
* It lists the exact potential displacements, each with a capacity-only,
  all-accept provisional replacement slot.
* Money is in two tables. Guaranteed: recruiting fee, weekly guarantee,
  earliest lawful exit cost, severance, contract pay to the last day.
  Forecast (labelled estimates): weekly contract cost no longer owed, and
  displaced bookings.
* It is pure: every digest is unchanged after any number of previews. Two
  worlds with the same past and different hidden futures give
  byte-identical previews (tested).

**Interfaces.** The same `api.Action` carries the new fields (absent on
V0.1 actions, which encode exactly as before). They reach the engine
through `Apply`, the CLI actions file (`run -actions`), replay, and the TUI
staff screen (`m`: registry, hire form, terminations, live preview).

**Deviations from the text above, identified:**
1. The guarantee provision stays in accrued payroll (Slice 1). Severance
   payable is its own liability, as §9 says. The baseline digest is kept by
   listing V0.2 accounts only when they hold a balance.
2. Severance and its benefits post to the one severance account, so
   severance is one identifiable amount. Benefits on wages are unchanged.
3. The result schema stays `dreamteam.result/v1`. Its new fields are
   omitted when zero, so V0.1 results are byte-identical. A `result/v2` with

4. A start earlier than the recruiting lead is refused as `lead_too_short`
   (a code not in §6.3's list).
5. A hire's name is its ID: §6.2's schema has no name.
6. The rest rule measures from the shift cap (the latest permitted
   release, at most 23:59:59). An evening shift ending at 20:00 therefore

7. The engine compatibility registry replaces the single mismatch switch
   for v1 records (§12; see `internal/session/record_v1.go`).

### 5.8 Slice 3 as built (0.2.0-dev.3): set_roster

The owner approved `set_roster` (2026-10-15) and nothing else:
`set_reading_allocation` and `contract_teleradiology` come later. Three
owner rules frame it: the contract never changes with the roster; roster
changes are future-effective, with the 14-day notice and Monday rules,
and past roster history never changes (saving and replaying included);
and every affected booking is revalidated by the existing displacement
lifecycle, once per batch, against the batch's final staffing. Without a
`set_roster` action every earlier outcome is unchanged (§11).

**Roster versions.**
* An hourly technologist's or front desk employee's roster is a timeline:
  the original roster (the scenario's, or the hire's, both immutable) and
  dated versions. A version is a complete A/B pattern effective from a
  Monday; it replaces its predecessor from that day until the next
  version. The A/B weeks stay anchored to the scenario's `rotaWeekA`.
* The version for any date is the latest effective on or before it
  (`termsOn`, behind the per-day registry view `workingTerms` that the
  plan, the runtime and every booking predicate already read).
* Versions are state in the employment record, in date order. `op: set`
  inserts a version, or replaces a pending one on the same date; `op:
  remove` deletes a pending one, and its predecessor resumes. A version in
  effect, or past, is never changed: its date is today or earlier, which
  every write refuses. Every operation, including replaced, removed and
  voided versions, is appended to the employee's roster log, so history
  is never lost.
* Several successive future versions are allowed, in one batch or many.
  Two writes to the same employee and date in one batch are
  `invalid_parameters`, whatever their order (a no-op counts).
* Radiologists and the operations manager have no editable roster
  (`wrong_role`). Salaried radiologist rosters are never modified.

**Validation**, in order, stopping at the first failure:
1. Shape (`invalid_schema`): `op` is `set` or `remove`; `employee` and
   `effective`; a roster for `set` and none for `remove`; no other field.
2. Employee: `unknown_employee`, `wrong_role` (hourly technologist or
   front desk only), `not_employed` (already left).
3. Effective date: `invalid_date`, `past_date` (today or earlier),
   `not_monday`, `notice_too_short` (fewer than 14 days ahead: from a
   Monday, the Monday 14 days later is the earliest), `beyond_horizon`,
   `not_employed` (on or after the end of employment, a termination
   earlier in the batch included).
4. `remove` of an absent version is `unchanged`.
5. The roster's shifts one by one, exactly as for a hire
   (`invalid_roster`, with the field path): times, cap, breaks, and
   assignments and covers in rooms of the employee's own skills (a front
   desk employee to the desk only).
6. A roster equal to the one already in force from that date (the
   batch's earlier items included) is `unchanged`, before the whole-roster
   rules.
7. The whole-roster rules, as for a hire: at most six working days in
   each rota week; net rostered time in each rota week at most the
   **contracted** weekly time (not the old roster); and the rest rule
   over the 14-day cycle. The employee's own original roster may always be
   restored, as an unedited legacy roster need not meet these rules
   (Morgan's scenario roster has 40 h in week A against her inferred
   39.5 h contract).
8. Once every item has passed, the composed timelines are checked as a
   whole (`checkComposed`), employee by employee:
   * no version on or after the end of employment, whatever the order of a
     termination and the roster change in the batch (`not_employed`);
   * the rest rule across every future version boundary: Sunday under one
     version, Monday under the next. Withdrawing a version can bring two
     versions together and is refused the same way (`invalid_roster`);
   * a hire still in training must complete it within 60 days of the start
     under the new timeline (`training_incomplete`);
   * every approved extension of the employee still holds on the new shift
     (`extension_conflict`, naming the date): a shift that day, the
     extension within its cap, before the close, and covering a room, the
     desk or supervision. These are extend_shift's own conditions. A trainee
     covers nothing, so an extension on a day now in training conflicts.

**The rest rule.** One rule for hiring, roster changes and overtime: at
least 10 hours between one day's **shift cap** and the next day's start.
The cap is the latest permitted release, min(start + `maxShiftHours`,
23:59:59). It is not the scheduled end, and it is not the end of approved
overtime. Approved extensions (extend_shift refuses one past the cap) and
hold-over (stopped at the cap) can never run past it. So no approval can
break the rule, and extend_shift needs no rest check of its own. The rule
is conservative: an 11:30 start (cap 23:30) forbids a start before 09:30
the next day, and a 16:00–20:00 shift (cap 23:59:59) one before 10:00. It
is applied within a pattern (week A Sunday → week B Monday, and B → A)
and across version boundaries. Exactly 10 hours is lawful (tested at the
day boundary).

**Contract.** Contracted weekly time, the rate, the accrual weights,
notice and severance never change with the roster: §5.2's guarantee pays
contracted time not worked. A cut roster, even to nothing, pays the full
contract (a full week at four days or zero hours pays Riley's 40 h × $36 =
$1,440 with benefits on it, tested), and severance is the same before and
after. No new compensation model and no contract amendment exist: a
transition that would need one cannot be expressed, and is refused.

**Atomic batches.** A batch holding a V0.2 action (now `hire`,
`terminate`, `set_roster`) holds only V0.2 actions. The items are validated
in input order, each against the state plus the earlier items; then the
composed timelines (above). One refusal refuses the whole batch and
nothing is committed. A valid batch then:
1. commits in input order: hires, terminations (Slice 2), roster
   versions;
2. moves every trainee whose timeline changed to its new productive date;
3. runs the existing reconciliation once (§5.7, `displace.go`) from the
   earliest affected day, against the final timeline. That one pass
   refreshes every materialized day's coverage and slot staffing,
   displaces what lost qualified coverage, and leaves added capacity open
   for booking. A coordinated exchange of coverage, such as two employees
   swapping Monday and Tuesday, therefore displaces nobody, in either input
   order (tested). Either half alone displaces 11 bookings in the test.
4. reconciles approved overtime: a conflicting approval has already
   refused the batch; a termination still voids approvals after the end,
   with a log line (Slice 2, unchanged), and now also the pending versions
   effective on or after the end ("Roster change voided", in the roster
   log; never resurrected).

A roster addition never changes an existing patient outcome: the days
reconciled are all after today, and a booking that was supportable stays
supportable when coverage only grows (survivor re-insertion with the same
predicates).

**Training.** A hire's training is attended exactly as rostered, so its
last training day is the first day, from the start, by which the composed
timeline's rostered time reaches the requirement. Days before the change
are unchanged, so they equal attendance. A roster change recomputes it,
before the first working day or during training. Capacity is never
granted before completion: the training view, and an invariant, hold
until the productive date. If training moves later, the days in between
lose the employee's capacity, and their bookings are displaced with the
cause `training_delayed`. These can include XR bookings that lose a
shared technologist. If training moves earlier, the days in between are
staffed and their slots open.

**Displacement causes.** A batch's cause lists its kinds of change in a
fixed order, joined by `+`: `employment_ended`, `roster_changed`,
`training_delayed`. A termination-only batch keeps Slice 2's exact cause
and text.

**Finance.** Nothing new is posted. Worked wages, overtime (the weekly
threshold and approved extensions), benefits, the guarantee provision and
its payday, notice and severance all run as before, on whatever the
composed timeline rosters.

**Preview.** `PreviewStaffing` composes the batch (versions, trainees'
productive dates, terminations, hires) without committing. It adds:
* per roster item: rostered time before and after against the unchanged
  contract, the interval it governs, a trainee's productive date before
  and after, and the approved extensions it keeps (with their new ends);
* the batch's coverage differences: template slots in the booking horizon
  made bookable and taken away, by modality;
* exact potential displacements, with capacity-only provisional
  replacements, as in Slice 2;
* money in two tables. **Guaranteed:** the weekly contract guarantee,
  unchanged by the roster. **Forecast** (labelled estimates): contracted
  time not rostered, which is paid as part of that guarantee and is not
  extra; approved extensions kept, at the base rate; and the displaced
  bookings.
* A termination preview lists the pending versions it would void.
* Previews stay pure and use only public facts (tested).

**Observation and interfaces.**
* `api.Employee.RosterChanges` lists the pending versions.
* `World.RosterPlan` (also on Session) gives an employee's whole
  timeline: the original roster, every version with its A/B rostered
  time, which is in force and which are pending, the qualified
  assignments, and the earliest lawful effective Monday.
* The action reaches the engine through the same Apply: the action API
  (`op`, `effective` and `roster` on `api.Action`), CLI actions files,
  session v2 save and replay, and the TUI roster editor (`m`, then enter
  on a roster row; TUI_DESIGN §4.9c).

**Neutrality and compatibility.**
* The state digest stays `dreamteam.state/v3`. Roster versions and the
  roster log are encoded only when an employee has one, introduced by a
  tag no length prefix can equal. A state without them encodes exactly as
  Slice 2's did.
* A genuine Slice 2 save, written by the 0.2.0-dev.2 binary
  (`testdata/sessions`), replays under this build with every digest equal.
  In that save `set_roster` was an unknown action, and a mixed batch was
  refused naming only hire and terminate.
* One observable message changed. A mixed batch is refused with a message
  naming this build's V0.2 actions, now including `set_roster`. The Slice
  2 golden reference has such a batch, so only its action and run digests
  differ (documented in `MIGRATIONS.json`; every outcome, and the log,
  ledger and state digests, are identical).
* Session records of earlier V0.2 builds replay in their own batch mode
  (§12).

**Deviations from the text above, identified:**
1. **The rest rule** is measured from the shift cap everywhere, as in
   Slice 2's deviation 6. It is now documented as the single rule for
   hiring, roster changes and overtime.
2. **The original roster may be restored** exactly even where it breaks
   the whole-roster rules (§6.4: unedited legacy rosters remain valid).
   Any other roster must meet them.
3. **Extensions are re-checked** with extend_shift's own conditions on
   the new shift (shift, cap, close, coverage). Break rules are not applied
   to approved overtime: the §6.4 break rule governs rosters, and
   extend_shift has never applied it.
4. **Coverage differences** are reported for the whole batch, not per
   item: an exchange's halves are only meaningful together.
5. **A version may take effect before a pending hire's start date.** It
   then governs from the start. "Within employment" is read as before the
   end.
6. **Removal (`op: remove`)** is built as §6.2 specifies. Removing a
   version already in effect is `past_date`, since its date is not in the
   future.

## 6. Five actions: API, validation and previews

### 6.1 Common contract and atomic batches

`api.Action`, `World.Apply`, `Session.Apply` remain the public action path;
`ValidateBatch` is added, with `Validate` a one-element wrapper. The present
implementation validates/applies sequentially (`internal/sim/actions.go:44`);
**atomic V0.2 planning is new behavior**, not an existing capability.

A batch containing any V0.2 action must contain only the five types below.
Mixing legacy actions rejects with `mixed_batch`; execute legacy policy/extension
batches separately at the same timestamp if needed. Legacy-only batches retain
V0.1 sequential rejection semantics, including under v1 replay (§12).

For a V0.2 batch: decode strictly; build a private proposed registry/timeline;
resolve existing entity references in input order; validate the final composed timeline;
compute whole-schedule reconciliation once; price obligations; then commit all
changes atomically. No intermediate roster swap may displace a patient. An
invalid item rejects the **entire batch**: that item gets its stable code, all
other items get `batch_rejected` with the failing index. Deterministic first
error order is schema, identity/role, dates, terms, composed roster, capacity.
No employee, contract, booking, posting, generation or event is committed on
failure. Action-attempt records are still appended, as in V0.1. References name
entities already committed before this batch; no implicit references to a hire
or contract created earlier in the same batch are supported. Hire includes its
complete initial roster; use its returned ID in subsequent batches.

Dates are practice-local `YYYY-MM-DD`; effects are 00:00 except immediate
acceptance obligations/reconciliation. Notice means calendar dates, not business
days. All requested effective dates must be within today's booking horizon
(default 120 days); subsequent recurring versions persist without a horizon
limit. `lastDay` is inclusive, employment ends at next midnight. Time passes
only after the batch. Same-timestamp events have already settled; commit queues
only events strictly later than now (`internal/sim/engine.go:229`).

Canonicalize weekdays, skills, covers and windows before semantic comparison.
An identical existing instruction is `ok:true, code:unchanged`; after basic
identity/shape checks it remains a no-op even when its original notice date is
now past. A no-op changes no operating state, costs, reservation, events,
generations or log; only the attempted-action record/digest grows. Empty batch
creates no record. A changed batch logs once per changed action and once per
actual lifecycle/displacement effect, settles once, and records assigned IDs.

### 6.4 Timeline composition, versions and extensions

For each employee/day: employment bounds → service-stage eligibility → latest
roster version effective on/before date → that rota week's pattern → approved

### 6.5 Previews and result contract

## 7. Whole-schedule reconciliation and patient displacement

### 7.1 Scope and deterministic survivor selection

`sharedFree` is an **insertion predicate** (`internal/sim/demand.go:273`), not a
validator for occupied slots. It assumes existing reservations are valid, can
skip unstaffed pieces, and can count an occupied proposed slot twice. V0.2 must
not call it independently on every occupied slot and call that reconciliation.

For every changed future day, collect the **entire affected booking set** across
all rooms, including transitive shared-technologist conflicts and all already
materialized days through the horizon. Rebuild coverage from final batch state.
Later unmaterialized days use the same plan accessor. There are no booked days
outside the materialized horizon; recall orders retain their original wanted
dates. Earlier days and patients already on site are untouched by these
future-effective actions.

1. Build a temporary reservation set with no future bookings. Recompute each
   slot's full-interval bookability, supervision and staff sets from coverage.
2. Survivor order: priority (urgent, soon, routine), original appointment start,
   original confirmation time, appointment ID. This is deliberately separate
   from the order used to offer replacement dates. No payer or contribution key.
3. For each candidate, test category, room/time, complete coverage, contrast
   supervision and insertion feasibility **against only retained reservations**.
   Search alternate qualified technician assignments for the entire conflict
   cluster before dropping this booking. At each constant-coverage segment use
   distinct qualified techs, deterministic ascending staff order and backtracking;
   allow the same segment-boundary handover as current booking. Do not commit a
   greedy technician choice that unnecessarily evicts another booking.
4. Retain feasible candidates at the original time; staffing reassignment does
   not change appointment or attempt identity. Candidates that cannot retain
   time become potential displacements. Recheck the final retained set as a
   whole, with zero-staff stretches forbidden even for non-shared slots. The same
   validator must cover initial future-plan construction and replacement
   insertions; it cannot be limited to the final newly inserted appointment.
5. Release **all** displaced reservations before offering any replacement.
   Reoffer using §7.2, checking each accepted insertion and the complete final
   reservation set. This algorithm is deterministic, not a maximum-throughput
   optimizer; survivor priority is a published allocation rule.

Desk removal changes registration waits/self-registration, not room-slot
bookability: current centralized booking consumes no desk labor. Preview that
service risk; do not invent a desk-based automatic cancellation rule.
Allocation-only changes do not alter on-site contrast supervision and therefore
do not cause imaging-booking reconciliation.

### 7.2 Displacement state, offers and identity

Add terminal appointment state `displaced`, and a separate immutable record:
`{id, order, oldAppointment, actionIds, planVersion, cause, displacedAt,
 originalStart, replacementAppointment, resolution, resolvedAt}`.
Causes include `roster_reduction`, `employment_ended`, `training_delayed`,
`extension_voided`; a multi-action batch records every contributing action in
input order and the first failed feasibility condition. The order enters
`reschedule_pending` until the offer resolves to booked or `lost_displacement`.
This is **practice-caused displacement**, not patient cancellation or no-show.

A displaced appointment retains its old date/room/slot as history but holds no
reservation. Slot owner, day booked count, order's current appointment and
invariants must be updated together. The old cancellation/arrival/grace events
are stale by appointment state and generation. Do not invoke `cancel`
(`internal/sim/demand.go:673`) or the arrival `reschedule` path
(`internal/sim/flow.go:232`): those have different counters, draws and slot
ownership. Historic appointments are never deleted.

Rebooking order: original scheduled start, then priority, receipt time, order
ID, displaced appointment ID. Search same-category replacement slots no earlier
than the old absolute start, original wanted date, and now+booking notice.
Thus a future patient is not unexpectedly moved to an earlier appointment.
Try preferred window then any window, with deterministic day/time/room/slot ties.
First attempt reassignment at the original time (§7.1); only then offer a date.

Use the existing patient preferred-window/any-window choice and tolerance
semantics, with the fixed anchor below; do not add an acceptance random draw.
Search the whole permitted horizon for a preferred slot, then fall back to the
first any-window slot when it meets the deadline. Preserve origin-keyed
preference/tolerance, including a walk-in's original conversion attempt.

Freeze a **tolerance anchor** when the patient's current voluntary booking
attempt starts: the original earliest date supplied to that attempt's offer.
All practice displacements in that chain use the same anchor and the same
patient tolerance; neither original wanted date nor deadline is renewed.
Acceptance requires replacement day ≤ anchor+tolerance. Failure records
`no_feasible_slot` or `replacement_declined` and the original referral/walk-in
origin. No replacement inside the current horizon is a resolved loss, not an
indefinite hidden queue. Ordinary recall remains reserved for original wanted
dates beyond the horizon.

Keep a separate monotonically increasing placement number for administrative
replacements. **Do not increment the behavioral attempt** on practice
displacement: preserve the original no-show, arrival-offset, cancellation and
exam/registration draw identity, including the original no-show probability
basis and confirmed-opening flag. New appointment ID and generation invalidate
old events; the same cancellation lead/arrival offset is applied to the new
start. If a cancellation timestamp would already be past, schedule it by the
existing future-time clamp, never before the action. Actual patient-initiated
cancellation/no-show retries increment behavioral attempt as in V0.1 and open
a new voluntary anchor. Read and claim draws remain origin-keyed throughout.

Offers resolve in the commit transaction with hidden acceptance evaluated only
then. Every displaced order reaches one observable resolution; future ordinary
patient events remain hidden. No new exogenous order or patient ID is allocated.
Counters distinguish displacement events, unique displaced orders, successful
replacements, repeated displacements and losses. Walk-in-origin replacements
never enter referral-loss numerators (`internal/sim/demand.go:192`).

### 7.3 Consequences retained

Keep receipt time, original wanted date, first appointment, all replacement
starts, displacement delay and current outcome. Do not erase patient waiting

booking is displaced. No fictitious revenue-loss journal entry or refund is
needed for an unperformed/unbilled exam. Financial previews label lost
contribution as a forecast; actual P&L follows actual service and postings.

## 12. Session v2 and authoritative v1 replay

New records use **`dreamteam.session/v2`**; native result output uses
`dreamteam.result/v2` and the state hash uses a `dreamteam.state/v2` domain. New records
include: the five action types and their operation variants,
atomic-batch markers, assigned identities, canonical action results, lifecycle
and displacement events, engine/state/output schema identifiers, scenario
fingerprint, seed, evaluation specification and checksums. Publish separate
strict v1/v2 DTOs and schema dispatch; do not expand the v1 unknown-field reader
in place. Preserve v1 field omission/order/checksum serialization rules. Keep
historical log, ledger and action hash domains/legacy action encodings unchanged: additive
V0.2 fields are absent on legacy actions/results. New action variants have a
specified canonical encoding; native result-schema metadata does not silently
change the legacy action digest. The run digest changes with the native state
hash; do not relabel an old digest with a new schema identifier.

The record's accepted/rejected action attempts and decision times are
**authoritative inputs**. Replay runs validation and simulation and compares
the resulting outcomes; recorded lifecycle events verify output, never instruct
the engine to skip recruiting, force a patient acceptance or inject money.
Native v2 replay requires matching engine semantics/schema and all five digests,
action results, event stream and evaluation spec. Rejections and no-ops are
replayed at their original batch boundaries.

The current reader rejects engine mismatch unless explicitly allowed, and then
still checks digests (`internal/session/record.go:371`). V2 must replace that
single compatibility switch with a documented engine/schema compatibility
registry. Only named V0.1 engine revisions whose legacy semantics have passed
§11 qualify for the v1 compatibility path; unrelated versions remain rejected.
Do not interpret a version number or a schema bump as proof of compatibility.

For a supported v1 record:

* Validate its original checksum/schema and resolve the exact original scenario
  fingerprint; preserve the v1 action DTO, messages, ordering and sequential
  batch semantics.
* Execute its recorded actions/times authoritatively through the legacy path.
  Compare recorded action results and the complete available legacy outcome
  projection; log, ledger and action digests remain exact.
* Report `outcomeCompatibility: verified` separately from
  `stateCompatibility: schema_changed` when native state/run hashes differ.
  Include both recorded and regenerated hashes; this is not "all digests match."
  A record without sufficient outcome evidence is `unverified`, not a pass;
  matched historical fixtures provide additional evidence, never fabricated
  missing record fields.
* Exact historical state/run verification requires its matching preserved engine
  build. The new engine does not promise to reproduce an obsolete state layout.
  Unsupported revisions fail with an explicit compatibility error.

*Slice 1 as built:* `session.Replay` treats records from builds with the v1
state encoding (`0.1.*` and the Step 0 builds `0.2.0-dev.0.*`), when the
engine mismatch is allowed, as follows. It verifies the action results, the
log, ledger and action digests and the summary exactly. It then reports
`StateChanged` ("outcomes verified; state encoding changed") instead of
failing on the state and run digests. Any other engine still needs every
digest to match. The session record schema itself is still v1, because
Slice 1 adds no action.

*Slice 2 as built:* this build writes `dreamteam.session/v2` and reads v1
through separate strict v1 DTOs (`internal/session/record_v1.go`): a v1 file
with any V0.2 field is not a v1 file. The engine compatibility registry
names the builds whose v1 records replay with outcomes verified and the
state encoding changed (`0.1.*`, `0.2.0-dev.0.*`, `0.2.0-dev.1`); it applies
without the mismatch switch, and only to v1 records. Any other engine
needs every digest (and the switch). A loaded v1 session saves as v2 with
`legacy`: the v1 record embedded (its own checksum still verifies), its
engine, checksum, last batch, end, the `legacy_sequential` batch mode, its
outcome and state compatibility, and **transition** — this build's five
digests at the prefix end, recorded when continued. Replay of such a record
replays the prefix in the legacy mode (outcomes verified), requires the
transition digests exactly, then applies the native batches and requires
all five final digests. Tampering with the prefix, the transition, batch
numbering or a native result is refused (tested). Reports name the
compatibility: `native_exact`, `legacy_outcomes_verified`, or
`prefix:…+native_exact`.

*Slice 3 as built:* records written by an earlier V0.2 build are a second
registry (`earlierV2Engines`, `internal/session/record_v1.go`): today
`0.2.0-dev.2`, whose action set was hire and terminate. Their state
encoding is this build's, so they replay **with every digest verified**
(`earlier_build_exact`), without the mismatch switch, applying their
batches with their own action set (`World.ApplySlice2`): there
`set_roster` was an unknown V0.1-path action and a mixed batch named only
hire and terminate, and both replay exactly. A session continuing one
embeds the whole earlier record as its prefix (schema v2, batch mode
`v0.2_slice2`, transition digests), exactly as a v1 prefix. A genuine
0.2.0-dev.2 save is a test fixture (`testdata/sessions`). Reports read
`prefix:earlier_build_exact+native_exact`.

Continuing an imported v1 session writes a **new v2 record**, preserves a hashed
v1 prefix and its legacy batch-mode descriptor, then appends native v2 batches.
Replay the prefix in legacy mode, verify the transition state against the new
engine/schema, and execute the suffix normally. Never rewrite the original v1
record or relabel its old state digest as v2. Public summaries distinguish
native exact replay, verified legacy outcomes and unverified compatibility.

## 13. Management evaluation: T90 vector and service gates

### 13.1 Windows, cohorts and endpoint closure

**T90 is primary**, measured from settled takeover time t0 to t0+90 days.
T30 is early operational/financial feedback, particularly while recruiting and
training costs precede productive capacity. T180 is optional longer-term
validation, not a substitute for the T90 gate. Preserve current posting-based
P&L and evaluation attribution; `internal/sim/evaluation.go:47` includes events
at the endpoint and captures the baseline after takeover's transaction.

Accept management actions only at decision instants **strictly before** the
chosen evaluation endpoint. Settle endpoint events/dispatch, then freeze the
management plan. Actions after that boundary belong to a subsequent period.
An action just before T90 retains its fees, liabilities, future roster and
termination effects. Publish the full accepted future timeline and minimum
unavoidable contract costs as of T90; do not value them at zero because payment
is later. V0.2 has no player action to defer a due payday, severance or vendor bill.

Run a fixed-policy **evaluation tail**, with no further management actions:

`tailEnd = max(T90 + 60 days, latestAcceptedEffect + 30 days)` **[proposed]**.

Here latestAcceptedEffect includes hire/training completion, termination end,
roster/allocation version and contract start/end; use projected completion after
composing all accepted rosters. Continue normal new demand, dispatch, accrual,
collections and settlement to expose delayed capacity loss. Tail service and
cash are additional columns, never merged into or substituted for raw T90 P&L.
An open recurring contract has no infinite end; include its current obligations
through tailEnd and its minimum notice tail. A long future booking remains on
the obligations/patient census beyond tailEnd. Unresolved due cohorts make
service eligibility provisional/failed, never a success by censoring them.
Optional T180 play with new decisions is a different experiment from this tail.

Use four separately denominated populations:

| Population | Identity / use |
|---|---|
| New demand | Unique referral and walk-in origins received in `(t0,T90]`, including refusals, booking loss and abandonment; retain modality, priority, source and payer strata |
| Inherited patient obligations | Orders/bookings outstanding at t0, separately tracked through completion, loss or tail; displacements never divide by only new receipts |
| Exam/report cohorts | Exams completed in-window, plus unread studies inherited at t0; report completion, overdue exposure and censoring stay attached to those cohorts |
| Attempt/event history | All cancellations, replacements, failed offers and repeated displacements; event counts cannot substitute for unique-order outcomes |

For throughput/access gates, a due order has original wanted/first promised
service time ≤ the evaluation boundary, never its delayed replacement date.
Apply the same rule to the paired no-action baseline. Show not-yet-due demand
separately; moving a promise cannot remove an order from the due denominator.
Preserve walk-in origin after a booking conversion. Do not reuse legacy
`LostRate` as a cohort success measure (`internal/sim/metrics.go:309`).

### 13.2 Visible metric vector

No weighted score. Display the vector, each denominator, the baseline difference,
gate status and uncertainty. Raw values remain visible on **every** failed gate.

| Dimension | Required fields |
|---|---|
| Operating performance | Raw revenue, expenses by account, operating profit/margin; contribution by modality; days 31–90; zero revenue gives undefined margin, not success |
| Cash / financing | T90 cash, minimum cash/date, A/R and A/P, accrued payroll, provisional guarantee, severance payable, due/past-due amounts, earned unposted wages, minimum future obligations and funding gap; inherited vs new attribution |
| Completed service | Exams and reports, separately; served fraction of all due demand including refused patients; by modality/priority and source/payer strata; inherited backlog resolution |
| Lost demand | Unique referral loss and walk-in abandonment/conversion; patient cancellation/no-show separate from practice displacement; unresolved orders and repeated displacement events |
| Access | Original wanted-to-first-booked, wanted-to-current-booked and wanted-to-actual-service distributions; p50/p90/max and unfinished current age, including inherited patients and displacement delay |
| Urgent TAT | Breach fraction/count, elapsed overdue study-hours, p90/max completed TAT, unfinished/overdue counts and oldest age; soon shown separately |
| Routine TAT | Completed p50/p90/max, unfinished count/age, overdue fraction and exposure, report-completion fraction; same fields for soon and each modality |
| Workforce obligations | Contracted, rostered and worked hours, training hours, scheduled-end overrun, weekly premium hours, break entitlement/taken/shortfall, maximum consecutive duty/rest deficits, payroll/notice/severance/retainer schedule |
| Reading mix | Employed/remote counts, costs, remote share, handoff and coverage-gap exposure, outcome tails by reader kind; no assumed quality equivalence beyond modeled measures |

Payer strata use only payer identities already disclosed through public
history; undisclosed payer stays in an explicit unknown group. Evaluation must
not draw or expose a refused patient's latent payer just to classify that loss.
Modality, source and priority coverage still uses the whole original cohort.

An unfinished study is right-censored at current age, not assigned a fictitious
completed TAT. Publish completed distributions **alongside** censored counts,
ages and overdue exposure; never let completed-only medians pass a gate by
starving the slowest cases. Runtime and calibration currently use different
TAT summaries (`internal/sim/metrics.go:441`, `internal/sim/calib_test.go:389`);
the new cohort report must make them comparable without rewriting old metrics.

Break entitlement is at least 30 minutes for a shift over six hours and no
uninterrupted stretch over six hours under the proposed roster rules. Count
missing **entitlement**, including unscheduled breaks, separately from existing
scheduled-break skips. Weekly premium hours start above 40 actual paid hours;
roster overruns measure a different quantity. Longer rostered shifts cannot
hide overtime or break deprivation. Genuine part-time hires can reduce premium
pay; that is an economic choice with hiring/training/guarantee costs, not itself
an exploit.

### 13.3 Baseline-relative minimum service gates

Let `B[m,s,h]` be the measured no-action baseline metric for scenario, stratum s
and horizon/cohort h. Step 0 supplies paired seeds and its 20-seed distribution;
the chosen scenario's no-action baseline is measured again with final contracts
and costs before release. Demand-weighted candidates use the matched-demand
reference specified in §4; a scenario cannot select a worse reference after
seeing an action plan. Store baseline version/hash and all thresholds in the
evaluation spec. No numeric service threshold is asserted by this draft.

For beneficial metrics use `L(m,s,h) = B[m,s,h] × (1 − DELTA_m_s_h)` and require
`X[m,s,h] ≥ L`. For harm metrics use
`U(m,s,h) = B[m,s,h] + DELTA_m_s_h × max(B[m,s,h], EPS_m_s_h)` and require `X ≤ U`.
`DELTA_* = PENDING_BASELINE_CALIBRATION` is a named nonnegative tolerance,
chosen from baseline variability and an achievable service envelope **before**
management-plan selection. `EPS_*` is a published measurement unit (one event
per fixed cohort for rates, one minute for delay, one study-hour for exposure),
not a hidden favorable target. An empty baseline stratum cannot automatically
pass: pool over the published seed set or mark it insufficient evidence and
require a declared aggregate/reference policy. No division by zero.

| Gate | Test and named threshold placeholders |
|---|---|
| Service volume and case mix | Due-demand exam served fraction and absolute completed exams ≥ L with `DELTA_EXAM_*`; same for every sufficiently supported modality/priority/source/payer stratum; fixed demand denominators prevent high-margin-only selection |
| Reporting | Due-report completion fraction ≥ L (`DELTA_REPORT_*`); unfinished overdue fraction and overdue study-hours ≤ U (`DELTA_UNREAD_*`); include inherited backlog and exams awaiting finalization at T90 and tailEnd |
| Demand retention | Unique referral loss, walk-in abandonment and combined practice-caused loss rates ≤ U (`DELTA_LOSS_*`); denominator is the original receipt cohort, with inherited losses in their own cohort |
| Displacement and access | Unique displaced-order fraction, repeat-displacement count, actual/current access p90, overdue waiting exposure and unresolved due fraction ≤ U (`DELTA_DISPLACE_*`, `DELTA_ACCESS_*`); original promise/tolerance anchors remain fixed |
| Urgent / soon service | Breach fraction, overdue exposure, unfinished oldest age and completed p90 ≤ U (`DELTA_URGENT_*`, `DELTA_SOON_*`); a zero observed baseline uses EPS and the calibrated tolerance, not arbitrary zero-breach perfection |
| Routine service | Routine completed p90, overdue exposure and unfinished oldest age ≤ U (`DELTA_ROUTINE_*`), plus reporting gate; median alone never decides eligibility |
| Sustainable coverage | Qualified jointly feasible booked/template capacity-hours and registration-capable duty hours ≥ L (`DELTA_COVERAGE_*`), by modality/week type through the tail; use baseline slot/open-hour denominators, not a reduced action-created denominator |
| Workforce | Weekly premium hours, break-entitlement shortfall and rest deficits ≤ U (`DELTA_WORKFORCE_*`); separately enforce legal-in-model roster/rest constraints and binding payment terms |
| Funding and obligations | Funding shortfall, late obligation amount/days and minimum cash drawdown no worse than U (`DELTA_FUNDING_*`) relative to the measured funded reference; no unauthorized borrowing, deleted liability or intentionally unpaid due bill can qualify |

**Owner's provisional T90 gates (2026-10-15)**, design thresholds, not yet
statistically validated. They are calibrated against the lead candidate
(urgent 10.1%, soon 27.3%, routine 4.2%) and the shared control (10.4%,
37.3%, 7.0%):

| Metric | Provisional gate |
|---|---|
| Urgent breach rate | ≤ 12% |
| Soon breach rate | ≤ 32% |
| Routine breach rate | ≤ 6% |
| Financial solvency | No missed mandatory obligation, no negative available cash |
| Patient displacement | Every practice-caused displacement accounted for; no silently lost appointment |
| Reporting backlog | No artificial endpoint deferral; outstanding work and its aging reported |
| Minimum service volume | Calibrated after dynamic action-plan testing |

Access losses, walk-in abandonment and TAT tails are reported alongside the
gates, so that a plan cannot improve breach percentages by avoiding
difficult cases. The dynamic implementation measures these across seeds,
with uncertainty and days 31–90, before they are locked. T90 is primary;
T30 is feedback.

A plan is service-eligible only when **all** applicable gates pass over T90 and
the defined tail checks. Aggregate gate thresholds are frozen from the measured
baseline distribution; report per-seed violations and paired confidence
intervals as well as aggregate status. Do not select a seed or hide rare failures
behind the mean. Before code, adopt `MAX_GATE_FAILURE_SEED_FRACTION` and
`MIN_EVIDENCE_COHORT_SIZE` from the sweep/owner risk decision (§17).

Financial success requires positive operating profit, acceptable cash/funding
and service eligibility, not merely a favorable margin among a few retained
patients. Raw P&L stays posting-based. A separate endpoint obligation bridge
adds earned-but-unposted wages and claim-committed remote fees, then lists
recognized liabilities and future unavoidable wages/severance/retainers without
double counting. Cash required to settle these obligations is compared with
available cash and explicitly authorized financing; latent receipts are not
certain funding. There is no new financing action in V0.2. Show a funding need
rather than invent a credit facility. A positive raw margin with unpaid or
unfunded obligations is labelled financially incomplete, not a management win.

Outsourcing passes the same report/access/quality and funding gates, plus actual
eligibility/coverage/concurrency constraints. More outsourcing is not inherently
bad: if it is cheaper and meets measured service obligations, the vector should
show that result. Excessive outsourcing that leaves paid in-house capacity idle,
misses urgent windows or creates unfunded retainers appears in cost, utilization,
TAT and commitment metrics; no fabricated price floor is needed.
