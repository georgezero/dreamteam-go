## 2. Independent review

**INDEPENDENT REVIEW: PENDING.** No independent reviewer has labelled the
98 items. Nothing was labelled by the developer or a model.

**What V0.9a adds** to make the review possible, without changing it:
* **[review.html](review/review.html):** one self-contained file, built
  from the frozen package by `analysis/evidence/review_tool.py`.
  * It refuses to build if the items differ from the manifest's SHA-256.
  * Offline: no network, no installation.
  * Per item it shows the identifier, recording, sources, reason with the
    span highlighted, observation (staff, prompt excerpt), proposed
    actions, validation and execution, what the rule checked, its output
    and the evidence completeness.
  * The reviewer chooses one of the protocol's four labels and writes a
    brief explanation (required for any label other than `supported`),
    and a correction for `unsupported`.
  * The independence declaration has three statements.
  * It saves `form.csv` (the frozen form's columns and rows) and
    `reviewer.json` (the protocol's format), and resumes from a saved
    form.
  * It shows no suggestion and no default.
* **`analysis/evidence/review_metrics.py`**
  (`dreamteam.evidence-review-metrics/v1`): with no form it writes PENDING
  and no figure. With completed forms it applies the protocol's checks and
  stricter ones, then reports (REVIEW_STATUS.md):
  * the number reviewed, and coverage by rule;
  * agreement with the rules' outputs;
  * supported findings confirmed, unsupported, ambiguous and
    insufficient;
  * precision per rule, with its denominator, the number of findings it
    was drawn from, and whether it is a census (A: 5 of 5; C: 3 of 3) or a
    stratified, non-random sample (D: 10 of 107; E: 10 of 45; F: 10 of 27);
  * disagreement between reviewers.

  It never reports recall.
* **[RECALL_PROTOCOL.md](review/RECALL_PROTOCOL.md):** why the sample
  cannot estimate recall, and a separate design (a random sample of
  sentences, labelled blind by two independent annotators) for the owner
  to decide on. Not run.

**How it was tested,** with synthetic labels only (generated by the tests
and discarded):
* The page was driven end to end in headless Chromium:
  * all 98 items labelled;
  * explanations with commas, quotes and new lines;
  * the declaration made;
  * both files saved;
  * progress kept across a reload.

  The saved files passed `review_analysis.py` and `review_metrics.py`.
* The page's own logic runs under Node in the test suite: the form
  round-trip, refusal of a changed row, the declaration.
* Metrics are refused for:
  * an incomplete form;
  * no declaration;
  * independence not declared;
  * a declaration not fully made;
  * another sample;
  * a label outside the protocol;
  * a changed rule output;
  * two forms by the same reviewer.

## 3. Parameter evidence: the rules

`dreamteam.parameter-evidence/v1`, a new contract beside the frozen v1. It
asks whether a parameter a reason states explicitly agrees with the
action submitted in the same reply. Specification:
[PARAMETER_EVIDENCE.md](../../PARAMETER_EVIDENCE.md).

| Rule | Stated | Against |
|---|---|---|
| `AMOUNT_MISMATCH` | an hourly rate for a hire; an allocation as % or BP | `rateCents`; `siteAllocationBP` |
| `TARGET_MISMATCH` | one named employee or contract ID | `employee`; `contract` |
| `DATE_MISMATCH` | an ISO date, a month and day, a weekday, today, tomorrow | the action's date parameter |
| `DURATION_MISMATCH` | an extension's length | `extend_shift.minutes` |
| `DIRECTION_MISMATCH` | an increase or decrease of an allocation | the value against the allocation in effect |

Every check carries:
* its recording, decision point and attempt (llm-agent 3) or "final
  parsed reply" (llm-agent 2);
* the reason span and the statement;
* the action reference (`point.attempt#index`) and its validation;
* the parameter, the stated and submitted values, and the normalization
  rule;
* the status, the evidence completeness and a stable identifier
  (`pe1-…`).

**What is not read at all** (statements excluded, per group, above):
* hedged, negated or rejected, or past;
* several action types at once;
* a reason that revises itself.

**Broad strategic statements** ("we need to cut costs") name no action
phrase and are never read.

## 7. Calibration: what the developer changed, and why

The rules were calibrated on this corpus, with no held-out set. The
developer read every `supported` check of each draft. That reading was
calibration, not a review.

1. **The first draft** read whole sentences. It made 1,234 checks, 13 of
   them `supported`, and all 13 were false:
   * descriptions of state: "Dr. Avery's site allocation is currently
     6000 BP (60%)", "the pending allocation change … takes effect on
     Oct 31";
   * names and dates in another role: "before Dr. Blake arrives", "from
     today (Oct 17)", "the existing … contract (T00001) which starts on
     2026-11-01";
   * a capability: "Dr. Avery can extend up to 19:30 … (210 min) is max".

   → Statements became action phrases: an intention-anchored verb, up to
   a subordinate break.
2. **The second draft** had 7, all false:
   * six gerund subjects rejected in the predicate ("Extending Dr.
     Blake's shift **is not feasible / is blocked / is invalid**");
   * one hedged statement about an action not submitted ("I will also
     extend Dr. Blake's shift … if needed", beside "I will extend Dr.
     Avery's shift by 120 minutes").

   → Exclusions became sentence-wide, with rejection words. A named target
   whose submitted action another statement names is an omission, not a
   mismatch.
3. **Distributive statements** ("Riley and Casey by 2 hours each", "both
   by 210 minutes") were bound to each action, which resolved 32
   abstentions.

**Two crude, independent scans** looked for missed mismatches:
* "extend … by N" where no submitted extension has N minutes;
* "I will extend <name>" where that person has no extension.

They found 1 and 9 candidates. Every one was a statement about a
previous attempt, or a hedged or revised statement about an action not
submitted. None was a mis-stated parameter of a submitted action.

Each corrected false positive is now a test. There are 40 tests in all.

## 8. Reproducibility

* The parameter evidence, run twice on the replays, is byte-identical
  (horo, linux/amd64). CI runs it twice and compares it with the
  committed file, and across linux/amd64, linux/arm64 and darwin/arm64.
* The review page and the PENDING status are rebuilt by CI and compared
  the same way.
* No model was contacted, and the whole-corpus test runs with sockets
  refused.
* Unchanged: every recording, the frozen v1 evidence, repeat v2, both
  refusal-response versions, the review package, every session fixture
  and golden reference, every benchmark fingerprint.

The quality gates are in [PROGRESS.md](../../PROGRESS.md) (V0.9a).

## 9. Known limitations

* **The independent review is pending.** No rule, v1 or parameter, is
  independently validated. No precision, recall or agreement is
  reported.
* **Calibrated on the corpus it describes, with no held-out set.** The
  parameter rules have found mismatches only in synthetic tests.
* **Narrow by design.** One hedge word excludes a whole sentence.
  Statements naming no explicit value are not read.
* **llm-agent 2 records only the final parsed reply,** so V0.5c–V0.7c
  support fewer checks per decision point than V0.8c.
* **Not covered:**
  * no recorded hire stated an hourly rate;
  * contract amounts (plans, retainers) have no action parameter to
    compare with;
  * direction is checked for allocations only.
* **English patterns only;** weekdays are compared as weekdays, not as
  specific dates.
* **No outcome is read and no cause is claimed.** The counts are not model
  scores.
