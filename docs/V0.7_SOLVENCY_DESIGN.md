# V0.7b design note: financial solvency and cash constraints

Status: implemented in V0.7b (0.7.0-dev.2) as designed, and released in
V0.7.0 (owner decision 2026-10-30). Owner decision for the design:
2026-10-29.
Objective: a practice cannot finance unlimited discretionary spending
when it lacks cash and credit. It can borrow within an explicit limit,
at a deterministic cost. When an obligation it has already incurred
cannot be financed, the shortfall is recorded, not hidden. No historical
result changes.

Conclusion: **a coherent design exists.** The ledger already separates
what the practice owes now (accrued payroll, payables, severance payable)
from what an action would commit it to. Each staffing action's preview
already lists the money it guarantees. Those two facts give a funding
test that preview and application can share. The model is switched on by
an optional scenario setting whose absence means today's behaviour.

## 1. What the engine does today

| Mechanism | Timing | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | earned at report final, at expected net | Dr A/R / Cr revenue |
| Collections | claim submitted after a 1–N business-day charge lag; payer remits after its delay; denials reworked | Dr cash / Cr A/R (billing fee to payables) |
| Salaries | accrued daily at 00:00 (radiologists × site allocation) | Dr wages, benefits / Cr accrued payroll |
| Hourly wages | accrued when the employee goes home; the weekly contract guarantee trued up at Sunday's close | Dr wages, benefits / Cr accrued payroll |
| Radiologist overtime (V0.7a, `salary_rate`) | accrued when the radiologist goes home | Dr wages, benefits / Cr accrued payroll |
| Payroll | paid at 00:00 every 14 days from the anchor | Dr accrued payroll / Cr cash |
| Fixed costs | accrued daily at 00:00 | Dr facility, equipment, IT / Cr payables |
| Supplies, per-study IT | at exam completion | Dr supplies, IT / Cr payables |
| Recruiting fee | at hire acceptance | Dr recruiting / Cr payables |
| Severance | recognized at termination acceptance, paid on the first payday after the last day | Dr severance / Cr severance payable; then Dr severance payable / Cr cash |
| Teleradiology | retainer accrued daily, read fees at report final | Dr remote retainer, reads / Cr payables |
| Payables | paid at 00:00 on the 1st | Dr payables / Cr cash |
| Opening | one adjustment at the takeover sets cash to `openingCashCents` | Dr cash / Cr opening equity |

Cash leaves the practice **only at 00:00**, on paydays (payroll and
severance) and on the 1st (payables). It arrives only with payer
remittances, on business days.

Cash may already go below zero. The engine tracks the lowest balance,
days ending below zero and the current shortfall, and raises one alert per
dip (SIMULATION_DESIGN §8.7). There is no credit line, no interest and no
consequence. The observation has carried `financing.creditLineModeled:
false` since V0.2.

**How close the five practices come.** Measured with the V0.7a `-ot`
scenarios on a seed V0.7a already used (41401, no action, decisions not
run), at 07:00 each day for 90 days:
* cash stays positive throughout (V0.7a's characterization: lowest
  $6,546 in 90 runs, under greedy);
* cash minus payables and accrued payroll starts at about $60,000 in every
  practice;
* in financial pressure it falls to $2,504 by day 90; in the stable
  practice it stays between $39,000 and $68,000.

So in these practices the limit on spending is the money already owed,
not an overdraft. A credit line is drawn only if cash would go below zero,
which none of them reaches in 90 days without new spending.

## 2. Committed obligations and discretionary expenditure

**Committed obligations** are what the practice has already incurred, or
is contractually bound to, without a new decision:
* accrued payroll, payables (including recruiting fees, retainers and
  read fees already accrued) and severance payable: **current
  liabilities**, on the ledger;
* salaries and fixed costs as they accrue each day;
* hourly contract guarantees, notice and severance of existing staff;
* the retainer of a teleradiology contract to its earliest possible end;
* approved extensions and accepted allocation, roster and hiring changes
  (once accepted, they are commitments like any other).

None of these is ever refused, cancelled or deferred by V0.7b.

**Discretionary expenditure** is a new management action that adds a
cost. The engine's actions, classified:

| Action | Adds a cost? | Commitment (what must be fundable) |
|---|---|---|
| `hire` | yes | the recruiting fee, plus the earliest lawful exit: the contract guarantee from the start to the earliest last day, and two contracted weeks' severance, with benefits. These are exactly the preview's guaranteed lines. |
| `set_reading_allocation` | when it raises a radiologist's salary | the salary increase with benefits for 30 days (the preview's guaranteed line) |
| `contract_teleradiology` start | yes | the retainer to the earliest end (minimum term and notice; the preview's guaranteed line) |
| `set_roster` | no: a roster cannot exceed the contract (`invalid_roster`), and the contract guarantee is owed whatever is rostered | none |
| `extend_shift` | when the time is paid | the extension at the overtime rate (×`overtimeFactorBP`) with benefits for hourly staff; at the salary's hourly rate with benefits for a radiologist under `salary_rate`; nothing otherwise |
| `terminate`, teleradiology cancel, allocation decrease, walk-in and worklist policies | no (they reduce cost, or have none) | none |

Termination creates severance, but it is not discretionary spending: it
ends a larger obligation. Refusing it would trap a practice in its costs.

Uncertain costs are not commitments. Remote read fees depend on volume.
Overtime beyond an approved extension depends on patients. Recruiting
replaces nothing. They remain costs when they happen.

## 3. Available cash and funding headroom

    available cash   = cash + (credit limit − credit drawn)
    current liabilities = accrued payroll + payables + severance payable
    funding headroom = available cash − current liabilities − reserved commitments

* **Receivables are not counted.** A practice can be profitable and still
  short of cash, because payers pay weeks after payroll is due. Unbilled
  work is never cash (V0.2_DESIGN's obligation bridge).
* **Profit is not counted.** The test is liquidity, not trailing profit. A
  practice with reserves can fund a commitment while losing money, and a
  profitable practice can be refused one.
* **Reserved commitments** prevent one balance from funding many
  commitments:
  * Each accepted commitment, except an extension, is reserved at
    acceptance for its amount. The recruiting fee is excluded, because it
    is posted to payables at acceptance.
  * The reservation is released evenly over the commitment's period, as
    its cost accrues:
    * a hire: from the start day to the earliest last day;
    * an allocation increase: 30 days from its effective date;
    * a teleradiology contract: from activation to the earliest end.
  * The amount still reserved on day `d` is `C − ⌊C × elapsed / days⌋`,
    where `elapsed = clamp(d − from + 1, 0, days)`.
  * A later reversing action (a withdrawal, a cancellation, a termination)
    does not release a reservation early, so the rule errs on the side of
    caution.
  * An extension is reserved while it is still to be worked: an approved
    extension on a later day, or today for an employee not yet gone home.
    The reservation is read from the approved extensions themselves, so
    one voided by a roster change or termination is released.

**The rule.** A batch is funded when the sum of its items' commitments is
at most the funding headroom.
* Items are taken in input order. The first item that takes the running
  sum past the headroom is refused, with `insufficient_funds`.
* A V0.2 batch is atomic, so every other item in it is `batch_rejected`.
* An `extend_shift` is checked alone, against the headroom left after
  earlier extensions (they are reserved).
* An item with no commitment is never refused for funding, even when the
  headroom is negative.

The check runs after every existing validation, so an invalid action keeps
its own code. `Validate`, `ValidateBatch`, `PreviewStaffing` and `Apply`
call the same function on the same state, so the preview's answer and the
application's are the same.

## 4. Credit availability

A scenario declares a **credit line**:
* `creditLimitCents`: 0 to $1,000,000. A limit of 0 is valid and means no
  credit.
* `creditRateBP`: simple annual interest, 0 to 5,000 basis points.

There is no credit scoring, no covenant and no variable rate. The limit
never changes during a run.

## 5. Borrowing and repayment

* **Draw.** At each disbursement (payroll, severance, payables), if cash
  would be below zero after it, the practice draws `min(−cash, limit −
  drawn)` on the line, in the same instant (entry `credit_draw`). Cash
  goes below zero only when the line is fully drawn.
* **Repay.** At 00:00 each day, after that day's disbursements, cash above
  zero repays the line, up to the drawn balance (entry `credit_repay`).
  * Collections arrive on business days, so the line is repaid the next
    midnight.
  * Repaying moves cash and undrawn credit equally, so it never changes
    the funding headroom.
* **Never beyond the limit.** The drawn balance is always 0 to the limit.
  Interest is a payable, not a draw.
* Before the takeover, and in every scenario without the setting, nothing
  is drawn or repaid.

## 6. Borrowing cost

* **Interest.** Each day at 00:00, before that day's disbursements, the
  practice is charged interest on the balance drawn through the day
  before: `round_half_up(drawn × creditRateBP / (10,000 × 365))` cents.
  It accrues to payables (entry `interest`, one per day, keyed by the
  day), so it is paid on the 1st with the bills.
* **Expense line.** Interest is an operating expense line of its own,
  "Interest on the credit line" (account `interest`). This keeps every
  statement's identity unchanged. It is reported separately wherever
  money is reported.

## 7. Financial distress

**Distress** is cash below zero with the credit line fully drawn: the
practice could not finance an obligation it had already incurred.
* **The obligation is honoured.** Payroll is paid, bills are paid and
  severance is paid, exactly as before. The unfunded part is the negative
  cash balance.
* **It is recorded:**
  * a warning line at the disbursement: "Financial distress: payroll
    $X not fully funded: credit line fully drawn ($L); unfunded $Y";
  * the existing cash-below-zero alert;
  * the observation's shortfall, lowest cash and days below zero;
  * the funding headroom.
* **It ends** when collections bring cash back to zero or above.

## 8. Operational consequences

* **The only consequence is the funding rule (§3).** No shutdown, no
  closure of rooms, no layoffs and no deferral of payroll.
* In distress the headroom is negative, so every discretionary commitment
  is refused. Actions that commit nothing, and actions that cut costs,
  remain available.
* Existing payroll, staffing commitments, contracts and previously
  approved actions are never cancelled.
* A refusal is an ordinary action result, with code `insufficient_funds`.
  The agent protocol is unchanged: the code is a string like the others.
* **Visibility.** The observation's `financing` section gains, only under
  the setting, a `solvency` object: the credit limit, rate, drawn and
  undrawn amounts, current liabilities, reserved commitments and the
  funding headroom. A staffing preview gains, only under the setting, a
  `funding` object: the batch's commitment, the headroom and whether the
  batch is funded.

## 9. Historical compatibility

* **Opt-in.** The scenario's `finance.solvency` object (with both fields)
  turns the model on. Without it there is no draw, interest, reservation
  or funding check, and the code paths do not run.
* **Fingerprints.** The field is omitted when absent, so every existing
  scenario's canonical JSON, and fingerprint, is unchanged.
* **State digest.** The solvency state (draws, repayments, interest and
  reservations) is one pointer, encoded only when non-nil (the
  `omitEmpty` mechanism of V0.2 Slice 3). The two new accounts are
  appended to the chart, so they are encoded only when non-zero.
* **Configuration.** The setting compiles into the finance configuration,
  outside the digest, unlike `st.pol` (V0.7a's lesson). A run without it
  encodes exactly as before.
* **Observation and results.** The new fields are omitted when empty.
  Historical observations, previews, result files and contract reports are
  byte-identical.
* **Versions.** The engine becomes 0.7.0-dev.2. 0.7.0-dev.1 is registered
  as an earlier build (batch mode `v0.7_dev1`, with a session fixture), as
  every earlier version is.
* **Suites.**
  * Suite `dreamteam-dev` versions 1 and 2 are unchanged.
  * The solvency variants form version 3, new scenario files that extend
    the V0.7a `-ot` scenarios. No prior scenario file is edited.

## 10. Deterministic replay

* Every new number is integer cents, computed from state at fixed points:
  * 00:00 for interest, draws and repayment;
  * the decision instant for the funding test.
* The rounding is declared: interest rounds half up per day; a
  reservation's release is floored, cumulatively.
* Entry keys are built from day and employee indices, so an entry cannot
  post twice. This rules out duplicate interest and duplicate draws.
* Nothing depends on processing order or wall-clock time. A saved session
  replays its actions against the same scenario (embedded in the record)
  and reproduces every draw, charge and refusal.

## 11. Scenario versions and characterization

* **Scenarios.** Ten new files extend the five `-ot` scenarios:
  * `<base>-ot-credit`: a $50,000 credit line at 10% a year;
  * `<base>-ot-nocredit`: no credit (limit 0).
* **Credit size.** $50,000 is about one payroll in these practices. The
  zero-credit variant brackets it.
* **Opening cash.** Opening cash and every other parameter are those of
  the `-ot` scenario.
* **Suite.** Suite `dreamteam-dev` version 3 lists the ten.
* **Plan.** [benchmark/v07b-dev/PLAN.md](benchmark/v07b-dev/PLAN.md),
  committed before any run on its seeds.

## 12. Limitations

* **Short-term commitments only.** The funding test uses the near-term
  committed cost (§2), not the whole future cost of a hire or a raise. A
  raise that is fundable today may still be unaffordable in a year.
* **A simple credit line.** No covenants, fees, credit scoring or rate
  changes. Interest is simple and daily.
* **No shutdown.** Distress has no consequence beyond the funding rule.
  Payroll is never missed, so staff never leave unpaid.
* **Certain costs only.** Estimated costs, such as read fees, overtime
  beyond an approved extension, or a new hire's later wages, are not
  reserved.
* **No early release of reservations.** A withdrawal or cancellation does
  not release one before its period ends.
* **Manager overtime is still unpaid** (owner decision: deferred).
* **Agent responses to refusals are unobserved.** The rule is tested by
  deterministic tests and the V0.7b baseline characterization. In the
  V0.7c frozen-agent evaluation (75 runs) no action was refused
  `insufficient_funds`, and one run drew on the credit line
  ([benchmark/v07c-dev/](benchmark/v07c-dev/README.md)).
