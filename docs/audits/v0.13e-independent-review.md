# DreamTeam V0.13e independent technical review

**1. Executive assessment**

**NO GO for protocol lock.** The core approach can support a narrowly defined experiment about immediate action changes, but the current implementation can report a change in its primary “final action” distribution when both arms submit exactly the same actions. The final-decision instructions also conflict with the system-level response schema.

I identified **2 BLOCKER, 6 MAJOR, 2 MINOR, and 3 NOTE findings**. Passing tests do not resolve these findings: several were reproduced through deterministic probes against the actual engine and workflow.

**Review independence:** I am an automated reviewer, not a human reviewer. This review does **not** satisfy the outstanding independent human-review requirement. I provide no human identity, signature, or approval.

**Baseline and integrity.** The local initialized repository’s HEAD is `67a3389345bf280197d35323b6ccf74dd368fae3`, rather than the original `94af15b`. I verified all **1,965 files** against the supplied `snapshot-94af15b.tar`, byte for byte, before and after the review. This establishes agreement with the supplied archive; it does not independently authenticate the unavailable original Git commit object. The repository remains unchanged and clean.

**Review and verification performed:**

- Read all 57 rendered presentation files, including every supplied inspect detail, the 12-item questionnaire, and the record-only appendix.
- Inspected the active rendering path, typed view, `DecodeAgentAt`, selection rule, workflow, driver, outcome analysis, protocol, and human-review requirements.
- Passed the Go alternatives, session, and agent test suites; 25 Python workflow/driver tests; review-package consistency checks; and manifest/tree verification.
- Ran additional model-free adversarial probes, with scratch artifacts outside the repository. No network model endpoint or LLM server was contacted.

I did not rerun the complete historical replay reconstruction, race checks, or cross-platform matrix. My conclusions do not assume those checks would establish experimental validity.

**2. Presentation neutrality**

The supplied single-termination example is substantially clearer and more balanced than a simple “savings versus zero cost” comparison. It distinguishes facts, estimates, continuing obligations, and unavailable information. Currency precision and count formatting are generally consistent. I found no explicit recommendation such as “best,” “optimal,” or “recommended.”

Nevertheless, neutrality is incomplete:

- The menu always begins with the proposal, then no action. A disclaimer that order conveys no preference does not eliminate anchoring or position effects.
- Adjusted candidates deliberately favor a **smaller, later, or less binding commitment**. This is a substantive candidate-selection policy, even though candidates are not ranked by financial value.
- With multiple actions, the four-candidate cap privileges omissions of earlier actions.
- Continuing obligations are represented unevenly across candidates.
- Some available capacity information disappears from the comparison.
- A reversibility statement is materially inaccurate.
- Teleradiology details emphasize contractual spending while compressing the historical basis and service context of the fee proxy.

These create plausible pressures toward retaining, delaying, or avoiding commitments in some comparisons, and toward selectively terminating in others. **No model behavior was observed in this review**, so these are identified mechanisms of possible bias, not measured effects.

The shared system prompt also says doing nothing “is often right.” That is a common baseline framing, not a treatment-only difference. It still matters when describing the experiment as neutral.

**3. Capacity/displacement clarity**

The exact S1-a termination presentation adequately distinguishes the two requested quantities:

| Displayed quantity | Defensible interpretation |
|---|---|
| 2,481 scheduled slots no longer staffed | Scheduled capacity removed within the displayed booking horizon |
| Zero displaced bookings | No already-booked appointments lose staffing |
| Zero of those slots booked now | Current booking occupancy, not future utilization |

The rendered text explicitly says the slot count measures capacity, not patients, orders, or revenue. It also identifies future demand and slots beyond the horizon as unavailable. See the [S1-a termination detail](/home/george/dt-audit-v013e-review/repo/docs/v13e/review-package/messages/S1-a-treatment-inspect-proposal.txt:11).

A careful reader can therefore understand that:

- Closed slots are not necessarily future patients.
- Zero displaced bookings does not mean zero operational consequences.
- Neither number predicts clinical harm or future demand.

The principal problem is **coverage**, not that particular wording. The corresponding capacity disclosure is absent for supported roster changes and can vanish when termination is combined with another staffing action. That inconsistency undermines the otherwise useful distinction; see **DT13E-04**.

“Displaced bookings with a feasible replacement by capacity” must also remain distinct from successful rebooking. The presentation appropriately states that patient acceptance is unavailable before commitment.

**4. Termination-versus-keep balance**

For the supplied S1-a example, retaining the employee does not appear free:

- Continuing contract guarantee: **$1,975 per full week**.
- Conditional severance on a later termination: **$3,950**.
- Severance recognized now under no action: **$0**.
- Existing contracted and rostered capacity: **2,370 minutes per week**.
- Existing accrued payroll and accounts payable appear in both alternatives.

Termination separately reports **$4,350** of contract pay through the last day, **$3,950** severance recognized immediately, its payment date, and the estimated weekly contract cost no longer owed. The [no-action detail](/home/george/dt-audit-v013e-review/repo/docs/v13e/review-package/messages/S1-a-treatment-inspect-no_action.txt:3) clearly makes later severance conditional.

These are different accounting quantities and time bases. They should not be summed into a net financial advantage without a common horizon and treatment of shared obligations.

The implementation does **not** generalize this balance reliably. A leave-one-out candidate can retain an employee while omitting that employee’s continuing guarantee and severance exposure. No action following a roster proposal also lacks the corresponding continuing guarantee. Thus, the good single-termination example is not sufficient evidence of system-wide balance; see **DT13E-03**.

**5. Information boundary**

I found **no demonstrated forbidden disclosure through the active v2 turn/detail rendering path**.

The meaningful protections are implementation-based:

- Comparisons are rebuilt and checked against the current decision point through `DecodeAgentAt`.
- The rendered view uses typed fields, fixed labels, constrained values, and explicit reference mapping.
- Neutral identifiers are bound to actions in the original proposal, rather than produced through unrestricted string replacement.
- Inspect responses deliver requested details, not the full record object.
- Internal record metadata and the separate appendix are not automatically appended to model messages.

I did not find an active v2 route that renders random seeds, state digests, evaluation projections, infrastructure metadata, raw comparison source text, or internal refusal codes.

Two qualifications matter.

First, this boundary concerns the new comparison presentation. The surrounding conversation still contains the original public-state prompt, prior assistant text, and validation-first material. A typed comparison view is not a sanitization guarantee for the entire conversation.

Second, **typed does not mean complete or semantically correct**. The inaccurate reversibility statement passes the typed machinery because the incorrect statement is itself allowlisted. The capacity omissions also pass verification. The boundary provides useful protection against unauthorized content; it does not prove faithful management interpretation.

**6. Control/treatment symmetry**

The shared presentation head and closing instructions are byte-identical in all twelve supplied context pairs. Both arms use the same underlying settings and validation-first workflow at a fixed decision point.

The remaining differences are:

| Aspect | Verified difference | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Presentation | Treatment inserts the menu and target-reference explanations | Necessary for the chosen intervention |
| Information | Treatment can receive verified details | Intended difference |
| Response affordances | Treatment can select named alternatives directly; control must reconstruct other batches through revision | Avoidable difference in decision effort |
| No-action path | Treatment can select `no_action`; control’s equivalent empty revision is rejected as `no_decision` | Defect, not a necessary difference |
| Calls | Treatment offers an informative inspect round | Part of the intervention; causally relevant |
| Message sequence | Inspection adds another assistant/user exchange before the final decision | Part of the intervention |
| Feedback | Control inspection receives “no details”; treatment receives requested details or an inspection-related response | Expected interaction difference |
| Retries | Shared retry rules, but different response paths and opportunities to encounter errors | Equal limits do not ensure equal experienced retry behavior |
| Context length | Treatment receives more text; inspection can add substantial detail | Unavoidable information cost, with attention and context effects |
| Timing | More calls consume more wall-clock time and budget | Relevant to partial coverage; simulated state is checked for stability |

Final validation is shared, and a validation failure does not silently repair the proposed actions.

These differences mean the experiment estimates the effect of **the alternatives interface as implemented**, including information, menu shortcuts, inspection, additional calls, and ordering. It does not isolate the causal effect of verified facts alone. Recording call counts does not remove this distinction.

There is also a shared instruction defect: the system prompt requires the original `reason/actions` schema, while the later user message requires a different final-decision schema. See **DT13E-02**.

**7. Action semantics**

The principal selection semantics are implemented as declared:

- `select` preserves original operations actions and uses the selected staffing candidate unchanged.
- `revise` replaces the entire reply, including operations.
- `abandon` submits neither batch.
- The selected or revised batches are validated again before submission.
- A state-digest check guards against a changed decision point.
- Analysis records execution separately and preserves partial application.

Operations and staffing are separate batches, submitted in that order. This is **not cross-batch atomicity**. A successful operations batch must not be interpreted as rolled back merely because a subsequent staffing application fails. Offline execution of submitted batches also does not establish longer-term decision quality.

All seven declared labels exist:

`original_retained`, `listed_alternative`, `no_staffing_action`, `other_batch`, `abandoned`, `invalid`, `no_decision`.

However, their implementation is not solely a classification of actions. Identical empty submissions receive different labels depending on the response route. The precedence rules make labels mutually exclusive in code, but do not make their interpretation an unambiguous action scale. This is the main experimental blocker, **DT13E-01**.

**8. Observable comparison**

The active workflow restricts citation credit to detail associated with its recorded inspection history. It rejects undelivered values, wrong values, and unsupported paraphrases.

That delivery guarantee depends on the trusted workflow maintaining the inspection history. The lower-level decision operation accepts supplied inspection metadata; it is not independently proving that a model received it. I found no path for final model JSON to replace that workflow-maintained history.

The metric can be satisfied by copying displayed values. A deterministic probe received `compared` credit by citing one differing slot-count row for two inspected alternatives, with an empty reason. This is consistent with the mechanical definition.

Therefore:

- Inspection establishes information access.
- A verified citation establishes reproduction of a displayed difference.
- Selection establishes a declared choice.
- Validation establishes engine acceptability.
- Submission and execution establish later, separate events.

None establishes meaningful internal comparison, reasoning, or learning. The documentation generally recognizes this limitation; reports must preserve it.

There is also a numerical-formatting defect: JSON number `2481` fails where displayed string `"2,481"` succeeds, despite the stated numeric-equivalence rule. See **DT13E-09**.

**9. Experimental validity**

The declared schedule is internally consistent: **nine historical contexts, twelve sensitivity members, two arms, 262 draws, and a maximum of 802 calls**. The allocation is 84 Gemma draws and 178 Qwen draws.

The design supports only a constrained interpretation:

- Historical branch points are selected from previously observed proposals, not sampled randomly from management decisions.
- The initial proposals are replayed. This studies reconsideration conditional on those proposals, not fresh end-to-end management behavior.
- Historical model coverage is unequal.
- The twelve sensitivity labels are not twelve unique inputs: S1-a and S6-a have identical prompts and proposals.
- Each sensitivity pair changes multiple visible facts. Differences between pair members cannot identify the effect of one particular fact.
- Three or five repeated draws provide little precision. Temperature zero does not prove deterministic outputs or independence between requests.
- A/A contexts make no live model calls because their replayed proposals do not trigger the turn. They test workflow symmetry, not stochastic model equivalence.
- Interleaving arms helps with temporal confounding, but does not guarantee model stationarity or independence.
- Optional inspection is post-treatment behavior. Comparing inspectors with non-inspectors would not isolate an inspection effect.
- Multiple per-category intervals are not simultaneous guarantees. Small-sample empirical total variation can be positive even when underlying distributions are identical.

The implemented interval calculation illustrates the uncertainty: if both arms retain the proposal on every draw, the 95% difference interval is approximately **−0.5615 to +0.5615 with three draws per arm**, or **−0.4345 to +0.4345 with five**.

The partial-run policy appropriately forbids complete-run comparisons and replacement draws. Its verification implementation is incomplete, however: aborted-draw artifacts are not analyzed and inconsistent partial-run metadata is accepted. See **DT13E-08**.

Replay can establish consistency with recorded responses. It cannot reproduce stochastic model generation or authenticate an archive’s origin.

The four-hour “hard limit” is also overstated: the documented guard does not bound a blocked engine operation. See **DT13E-13**.

**10. Scientific limitations and missing information**

After remediation, the defensible question is:

> For these fixed contexts, replayed proposals, models, settings, and interaction rules, does assignment to the alternatives interface change the distribution of immediate submitted actions?

That is narrower than an information-only effect and much narrower than improved management.

| Category | Information and treatment |
|---|---|
| **A. Missing information that makes the presentation misleading** | Known retained guarantees omitted from some candidates; available roster capacity changes omitted; lawful withdrawal restrictions replaced by an overly broad statement |
| **B. Missing information that can be explicitly disclosed** | Future demand and utilization; future revenue and margin effects; replacement acceptance; consequences beyond the booking horizon; unavailable benefit estimates; limited history underlying fee proxies |
| **C. Outside intended simulator scope** | Real clinical harm, morale, reputation, resignation behavior, and broader organizational consequences not represented by the simulator |

The phrase “workload … not modeled” is broader than ideal because the engine does model operational work and capacity. Human workload, fatigue, or welfare should be distinguished from those simulated quantities.

An improvement claim would require additional evidence: a prespecified quality criterion, explicit tradeoffs, suitable outcome evaluation, and broader context coverage. Neither changed actions nor increased citations supplies that evidence.

The v13e documents generally disclaim improvement and single-fact causality appropriately. Remaining overclaims concern the primary action scale, partial-run reconstruction, the hard time limit, and the assertion that three draws can reveal whether a unit varies at all.

**DT13E-01 — BLOCKER — Identical actions can produce maximal primary-distribution differences**

**Location:** [turn.go:644](/home/george/dt-audit-v013e-review/repo/internal/alternatives/turn.go:644), [arms.go:274](/home/george/dt-audit-v013e-review/repo/internal/alternatives/arms.go:274), [v13d.py:797](/home/george/dt-audit-v013e-review/repo/analysis/alternatives/v13d.py:797), [OUTCOME_DEFINITIONS.md:3](/home/george/dt-audit-v013e-review/repo/docs/v13e/OUTCOME_DEFINITIONS.md:3).

**Evidence and failure example:** Against S1-a:

| Reply | Reported final category | Submitted batches |
|---|---|---|
| Control: `{"decision":"abandon"}` | `abandoned` | `[]` |
| Treatment: `{"decision":"select","id":"no_action"}` | `no_staffing_action` | `[]` |
| Control: `{"decision":"revise","actions":[]}` | `no_decision` | `[]` |

Five copies of the first two outcomes yield **category total variation 1.0**, while exact-batch total variation is **0.0**.

**Why it matters:** The declared action-distribution effect can be entirely a response-path effect. The empty revision also contradicts the interpretation of `no_decision` as no usable reply.

**Smallest recommended fix:** Use the already-computed exact-batch distribution as the action estimand; keep process status separate. Accept deliberate empty revisions consistently. If the seven-way scale remains primary, reconcile operationally equivalent no-action choices across arms first.

**Human re-review:** Required for changed response instructions or semantics; technical and protocol re-review required regardless.

**DT13E-12 — NOTE — Small-sample and context-diversity claims need tighter limits**

**Location:** [PROTOCOL_DRAFT.md:84](/home/george/dt-audit-v013e-review/repo/docs/v13d/PROTOCOL_DRAFT.md:84), [S1-a context](/home/george/dt-audit-v013e-review/repo/docs/benchmark/v13d-dev/contexts/contexts.jsonl:10), [S6-a context](/home/george/dt-audit-v013e-review/repo/docs/benchmark/v13d-dev/contexts/contexts.jsonl:20).

**12. Recommended remediation**

Recommended order:

1. Resolve the action estimand and contradictory response schemas.
2. Correct retained obligations, capacity disclosures, and withdrawal conditions.
3. Separate eligibility, exposure, process status, submitted action, and execution in analysis.
